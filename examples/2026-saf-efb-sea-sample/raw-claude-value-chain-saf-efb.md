## Stage 1: EFB Aggregators

### Description

Companies that collect Empty Fruit Bunch (EFB) waste from palm oil mills, perform pre-processing (drying, sizing, baling), and supply downstream processors. This stage sits between palm millers (who treat EFB as low-value byproduct or in-mill biomass) and SAF refiners (who need consistent feedstock specs).

### Activities

1. Source EFB from palm oil mills under offtake agreements
2. Pre-process: drying (60-65% moisture → <20%), shredding, baling
3. Storage at aggregation hub (handle ~3-month feedstock buffer)
4. Logistics from aggregation hub to refiner / pellet facility
5. Quality assurance: moisture, calorific value, ash content per buyer specs

### Revenue Model

Volume-based feedstock supply contracts (USD/ton dry basis), priced 30–60% premium over wet EFB at mill gate to cover pre-processing and logistics. Some integrated players also earn carbon credit revenue under voluntary markets.

### Cost Drivers

Mill-gate EFB price (often near-zero or tipping fee), drying energy (often biomass self-supply), shredding/baling labor and equipment, trucking from mill to hub, certification costs (ISCC EU for export-grade SAF feedstock).

### Preliminary Pain Points (this stage)

- Logistics cost from dispersed mills to aggregation hub represents 30–50% of delivered EFB cost (5)
- High moisture content (~65% at mill gate) requires drying infrastructure that small aggregators lack (1)
- ISCC EU certification expensive (~USD 50–100K initial + recurring audits) for export-grade SAF feedstock (6)
- Volume commitments to SAF refiners (typically 100K+ tonnes/year) exceed most aggregators' single-source capacity
- Competition from biomass pellet export market (Japan, Korea utilities) (4)
- No standardized EFB grading system creates buyer-seller information asymmetry

## Stage 2: SAF Refiners (HEFA + ATJ pathways)

### Description

Operators of refining facilities that convert biomass feedstock (including EFB-derived intermediates) into Sustainable Aviation Fuel meeting ASTM D7566 specs. Two main pathways relevant to EFB: HEFA (Hydroprocessed Esters and Fatty Acids — uses palm oil derivatives, EFB enters indirectly via lignin valorization) and ATJ (Alcohol-to-Jet — uses cellulosic ethanol from EFB).

### Activities

1. Feedstock procurement and quality assurance (multi-source blending common)
2. Conversion processes: HEFA hydrogenation OR ATJ fermentation + dehydration + oligomerization
3. Distillation and blending to ASTM D7566 specs
4. Certification: ISCC EU + CORSIA-eligible feedstock chain of custody
5. Offtake agreements with airlines / petroleum jobbers

### Revenue Model

SAF priced as premium over fossil jet fuel — typically USD 1,500–2,500/ton SAF vs USD 700–900/ton fossil jet (2024 averages) (1). Premium captured via airline mandates (EU SAF mandate from 2025, Singapore from 2026), corporate scope-3 commitments, and book-and-claim REC trading.

### Cost Drivers

Feedstock (typically 50–70% of opex), hydrogen (HEFA pathway), capital cost amortization (USD 500M–2B per facility), certification, blending logistics.

### Preliminary Pain Points (this stage)

- Feedstock supply uncertainty: SAF refiners need multi-million-ton/year guarantees, dispersed EFB aggregators struggle to commit
- High capex (USD 500M–2B per facility) creates financing barriers in SEA where green-finance markets are nascent
- ICAO CORSIA-eligible feedstock chain of custody adds documentation burden, especially for palm-derived inputs given EU sustainability concerns
- Competition for hydrogen (HEFA pathway) where SEA hydrogen infrastructure is limited
- Mandate volumes still small in SEA (Singapore 1% from 2026), creating "chicken and egg" problem for facility investment

## Cross-stage observations

- Feedstock-to-refiner volume mismatch is the structural friction: aggregators typically operate at 100K+ tonnes/year; refiners need 1M+ tonnes/year. Bridging this requires either consolidation (M&A in aggregator stage) or feedstock pooling models.
- Certification chain (ISCC + CORSIA + scheme-specific palm sustainability) adds 6–12 month lead time and meaningful cost — likely a Stage 3 (intermediary / certifier) opportunity not yet mapped here.
