## Stage 5: Existing Preventive / CDM Delivery Landscape

### Pain point 5.1

**Title:** Private hospitals are revenue-optimized for high-intensity episodic events, not longitudinal CDM — the fee-for-service architecture structurally rewards deterioration over prevention.

**Tests WH:** WH1 (affluent under-served — supply-side lens: the structural absence is not accidental but incentive-driven) — PRIMARY. Refined WH5 (providers, not just insurers, cannot deliver longitudinal CDM — SUPPORTED with structural mechanism).

**Description:** Indonesia's three largest IDX-listed private hospital groups — Siloam (SILO, Rp 12.2T FY2024), Mitra Keluarga (MIKA, Rp 4.87T, EBITDA margin 37.7%), and Medikaloka Hermina (HEAL, Rp 6.72T) — generate revenue exclusively through the per-visit, per-procedure, per-admission billing model. No enrolled longitudinal CDM programme exists, and none appears as a revenue line in any IDX disclosure. The incentive misalignment is direct: a MIKA patient whose hypertension is managed to target by a GP-anchored CDM programme and who never requires hospitalization generates zero MIKA revenue; the same patient who presents with a hypertensive crisis requiring five inpatient days contributes approximately Rp 2.0M per inpatient day to MIKA's Rp 3.26T inpatient revenue stream. MIKA's 37.7% EBITDA margin is structurally produced by this case-intensity model — inpatient revenue accounts for 67% of total FY2024 revenue, specialist-driven, high-acuity admissions. None of the four IDX-listed hospital groups (SILO, MIKA, HEAL, SRAJ) discloses a CDM patient count, CDM revenue line, or CDM outcome metric in their annual reports or quarterly press releases. The slot is not merely unfilled — it is economically invisible to hospital-group management under fee-for-service.

**Who bears it:** Urban affluent / upper-middle (MC2) patients with pre-diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidemia, or metabolic syndrome who attend premium private hospitals but receive no proactive longitudinal management between specialist visits. Also: private hospital groups themselves, whose high-acuity patient populations carry unmanaged NCD burden that will eventually drive hospitalisation cost exposure — though under fee-for-service, this cost falls on insurers or OOP-paying patients rather than the hospital.

**Estimated cost:**

- **NCD complication cost per patient — hospitalization premium:** Direct medical cost for Type 2 Diabetes with complications in Indonesia averages $930 per patient per year versus $421 for diabetes without complications — a 2.2x differential; of that total, 56% is spent on hospitalisation ($521 per patient per year for complicated vs $236 for uncomplicated). For Indonesia's estimated 18.69 million diabetes patients in 2020, rising to 40.7 million by 2045, the avoidable hospitalisation cost attributable to unmanaged complications is in the tens of billions of rupiah per year at the cohort level. (1, 2)
- **Provider revenue foregone — CDM patient-year premium:** No Indonesian provider publishes CDM patient-year revenue. [ASSUMED-1: A longitudinal CDM programme at the affluent price point — GP anchor, quarterly lab, medication management — priced at Rp 2–5M/month = Rp 24–60M/year (~$1,500–$3,700) represents 4–8x Mitra Keluarga's implied revenue per outpatient visit (Rp 388K FY2024 average) and 2–4x SILO's historical per-visit figure (Rp 1.2–1.5M, 2021–2022). An enrolled CDM population of 5,000 patients would generate Rp 120–300B/year in recurring revenue — a meaningful fraction of MIKA's Rp 1.61T outpatient revenue, captured on recurring rather than transactional basis. — to validate: estimate CDM programme revenue against actual tariff modelling with a private hospital CFO or insurtech actuary.] (3, 4)
- **National NCD hospitalisation cost burden:** JKN accumulated a $2.17B deficit by 2023, with chronic NCD hospitalisation claims growing at double-digit volume YoY. Indonesia's total direct medical cost for Type 2 DM alone was estimated at $576M total, with the uncontrolled/complicated cohort driving disproportionate hospitalisation spend. (5, 6)

**Willingness to pay:** [Data gap] No published survey on Indonesian private hospital groups' WTP to acquire a CDM patient population. From a corporate strategy standpoint, the question for incumbents is whether to build or partner — and the evidence suggests neither is underway. [Interview placeholder: ask MIKA or Siloam investor relations / strategic development about whether CDM recurring revenue is on the FY2025–2026 strategy agenda.]

**Frequency:**

- Zero of four IDX-listed private hospital groups (SILO, MIKA, HEAL, SRAJ) discloses a CDM programme in FY2024 annual reports or investor disclosures. (3, 4, 7, 8)
- All 16 private sector providers in Stage 5 taxonomy classified as episodic or transactional — none classified as longitudinal CDM at scale (see Stage 5 Episode-vs-Longitudinal taxonomy). (Stage 5 VC, Section 2)

**Root Causes**

- **RC1: Fee-for-service billing architecture creates a structural disincentive for hospital groups to invest in longitudinal CDM because prevented hospitalizations reduce, not grow, revenue** — Under fee-for-service, every inpatient admission is revenue; every outpatient specialist visit is revenue; but a patient whose DM is managed in a structured GP-anchored CDM programme and who avoids a 5-day admission generates no hospital revenue during the prevention interval. MIKA's 67% inpatient revenue share and 37.7% EBITDA margin are structurally produced by case intensity, not preventive throughput. Value-based care contracts that could realign this incentive (capitation-at-risk, shared-savings, bundled payment) do not exist in Indonesia's private-pay commercial segment. Performance-based capitation in the BPJS public channel (KBK, since 2015) has achieved only 4.1% of its chronic disease control target after ten years — confirming that even well-designed incentive modifications face structural implementation constraints in Indonesia's provider landscape. (3, 5, 11, 12)

- **RC2: No private hospital group has access to enrolled longitudinal patient data, making CDM programme design commercially impossible even if leadership wanted to build one** — A CDM programme requires knowing: (a) which outpatients have uncontrolled DM or HTN; (b) which had an abnormal MCU result with no follow-up; (c) which have deteriorating lab trends. SILO's 4.24M annual outpatient visits, MIKA's 4.15M, and HEAL's 8.0M are episodically recorded in per-visit EMR entries. No Indonesian private hospital has announced SatuSehat longitudinal patient linkage across visits in a CDM-navigable dataset. SATU SEHAT (national health data platform) reached only ~43% health facility adoption by October 2024, with data completeness described as "suboptimal." Without a longitudinal patient registry, the hospital cannot identify its own CDM-addressable population. (4, 7, and Stage 4 source 15)

- **RC3: The GP layer in Indonesia's private hospital system is absent or structurally suppressed — all private hospital outpatient revenue flows through specialists, making GP-anchored longitudinal care structurally incompatible with the business model** — Siloam, Mitra Keluarga, and Mayapada are specialist-centric: the outpatient income is generated by cardiologists, endocrinologists, and surgeons on per-visit fees. A GP functioning as a longitudinal primary care anchor would reduce specialist referral volume and thus specialist revenue. The patient seeking diabetes management returns to the endocrinologist quarterly — not to a GP care coordinator. In the public/BPJS system, primary care GPs are BPJS-capitation-funded at a flat rate that does not reward CDM outcome quality (KBK has failed to change this after ten years). The structural consequence is that GP-as-longitudinal-anchor is economically invisible in both the premium private and BPJS-primary channels. (12, 5, 9)

- **RC4: MCU is designed and priced as a one-time diagnostic transaction, not as the entry point to an enrolled care pathway — the structural absence of post-MCU care coordination is a revenue model choice, not an operational oversight** — Mayapada's MCU Climber and Spesial packages, RSPI's executive MCU, and Prodia's PHC screening packages all deliver a results report, not a care plan. Mayapada's 31,753 MCU patients in FY2024 were not enrolled in a follow-up CDM pathway — there is no GP assigned to manage their abnormal findings longitudinally. This is structurally rational for a provider that bills per-visit: each abnormal MCU result that generates a specialist referral is incremental per-visit revenue from the same patient, without the operational complexity of running an enrolled programme. The MCU market ($656M nationally in 2024, CAGR 9.9%) is growing as a screening and corporate-compliance transaction, not as a CDM entry gateway. (8, Stage 5 VC source 26)

- **RC5: The dual-speed market identified by L.E.K. has attracted premium hospital investment into high-acuity specialty (oncology, cardiac catheterization labs, robotic surgery) rather than preventive infrastructure — creating a capital allocation lock-in away from CDM** — L.E.K.'s 2025 analysis explicitly identifies Indonesia's premium hospital investment thesis as "repatriating outbound medical tourism" through advanced cancer care, cardiac catheterization labs, proton therapy (the first Indonesian center is in development), and 3.0T MRI — not preventive CDM. Capital follows the investment thesis. A Rp 50B investment in a proton therapy machine serves a high-acuity oncology patient who cannot be served anywhere else in Indonesia; a Rp 50B investment in a CDM programme serves a diabetic patient who today receives no service but generates no hospital revenue either. The capital allocation reinforces episodic specialization at the premium end. (12)

### Pain point 5.2

**Title:** Digital health platforms (Halodoc, Alodokter, Good Doctor/WhiteCoat) are structurally optimized for user acquisition and episode-by-episode consultation throughput — their architecture makes longitudinal CDM delivery commercially unviable within their current model.

**Tests WH:** Refined WH5 (providers also don't deliver longitudinal CDM — SUPPORTED: digital platforms are a specific provider subtype, and the structural mismatch is model-level, not operational). WH1 (supply-side gap is structural — SUPPORTED with digital channel lens).

**Description:** Indonesia's leading digital health platforms — Halodoc (20M+ MAUs, $258M raised), Alodokter (30M+ MAUs, claimed profitable 2023), and Good Doctor/WhiteCoat (combined 6.8M insured lives post-October 2024 acquisition) — are architecturally patient-initiated, episode-triggered, and monetised on a per-consultation basis. The user opens the app because of a symptom today; the platform generates revenue from that consultation; the user closes the app and is not proactively recalled. There is no enrolled chronic-disease cohort, no proactive outreach to a managed hypertensive patient who has not consulted in 90 days, no medication titration pathway, no laboratory coordination for quarterly HbA1c, and no care coordinator. JMIR research on telemedicine adoption for chronic disease management in Indonesia (2026) identifies a core structural barrier: "only a few [telemedicine CDM pilots] have been embedded in everyday practice, with many projects discontinued at the end of their pilot studies." ThinkWell's 2025 policy mapping of Indonesia's telemedicine landscape confirms that integration between telemedicine episodes and longitudinal care coordination is absent, with persistent system fragmentation (Mobile JKN app vs P-Care information system incompatibilities that "result in undercounted telemedicine encounters"). The digital health platforms, in aggregate, add to the episodic consultation count without adding to longitudinal CDM capacity.

**Who bears it:** Urban affluent / MC2 chronic disease patients who use telemedicine for acute or semi-acute episodes but have no longitudinal digital CDM platform serving them through the gaps between episodes. Also: the platforms themselves, whose post-COVID user growth has plateaued and whose unit economics require either a shift toward higher-LTV recurring services or continued high-CAC episodic acquisition.

**Estimated cost:**

- **CAC vs. CDM LTV mismatch:** Halodoc raised $100M in its 2023 Astra Digital round on a 20M+ MAU base — implied CAC of approximately $5/user at that base (rough order-of-magnitude; no published figure). Episodic telemedicine consultations in Indonesia are priced at Rp 20,000–Rp 150,000 per session (free for insurer-cashless users; Rp 50–80K for OOP). At 5–10 consultations per year per MAU (typical telemedicine utilisation), revenue per active user per year is Rp 250K–1.5M (~$15–$93). A CDM-enrolled patient paying Rp 2–5M/month generates $1,500–$3,700/year — 16–250x episodic per-user revenue. The platform that can transition a chronic disease patient from episodic consultation to an enrolled CDM subscription would achieve a structural LTV multiple. No current Indonesian digital health platform has announced such a product. [ASSUMED-2: Halodoc CAC estimated at $5–15/user based on $100M Series D on 20M MAU base; no published figure. — to validate: review Halodoc investor materials or ask Astra Digital IR about unit economics disclosed in the 2023 round.] (13, 14)
- **Post-COVID user retention collapse:** Indonesia's telemedicine market surged from 2M users in 2019 to 20M in 2020 (COVID-driven). Post-pandemic, all major platforms have reported slowing MAU growth and retention challenges as non-emergency use cases dissipated. Alodokter claimed profitability only in 2023, after seven years of operation — implying sustained cash burn from high-CAC episodic acquisition without sufficient LTV to break even earlier. The unit economics of episodic-only telemedicine cannot sustain the CDM infrastructure investment (care coordinators, protocol development, lab integration, physician time for longitudinal monitoring) without a structural pricing shift. (14, 15)
- **Revenue per episode — structural ceiling:** Bain & Company (APAC Front Line of Healthcare 2024) cites teleconsultation as "3x cheaper than offline" in Indonesia — consistent with Rp 50–150K per episode vs. Rp 300–600K in-clinic. This pricing is appropriate for acute episodic use but cannot fund the care coordinator, proactive monitoring, or medication management infrastructure of a CDM programme. The consultations are priced for a three-minute encounter, not a 30-minute care plan review with lab result integration. (16)

**Willingness to pay:** [Data gap] No published survey on Indonesian telemedicine platform WTP to acquire CDM product infrastructure. Halodoc's 2023 Astra Digital round prospectus is not publicly disclosed; CDM product development was not publicly announced. [Interview placeholder: ask Halodoc or Alodokter chief product officer whether CDM subscription product is on the 2025–2026 roadmap and what the clinical infrastructure investment estimate is.]

**Frequency:**

- Zero of six major digital health platforms (Halodoc, Alodokter, Good Doctor/WhiteCoat, Klikdokter, SehatQ, Doctor Anywhere Indonesia) has announced an enrolled longitudinal CDM product for Indonesian self-pay users. (Stage 5 VC, Section 1.4, 1.6)
- 91% of APAC consumers prefer a single touchpoint for managing their health (Bain 2023) — yet no Indonesian platform offers the integrated preventive + CDM + specialist coordination that would satisfy this preference. (16)
- Telemedicine CDM pilots in Indonesia have been systematically discontinued post-pilot rather than embedded in practice. (17)
- Indonesia holds 26.4% of SEA telehealth market by volume in 2024 — the largest share — yet all that volume is episodic. (18)
- Only 48.2% of healthcare providers always adhere to national CDM guidelines; 35.7% only frequently; and 28.6% rarely conducted kidney function monitoring for diabetic patients (Jakarta primary care study, 2022–2023). (9)

**Root Causes**

- **RC1: Platform revenue architecture is transactional per episode — a CDM subscription product that charges Rp 2–5M/month while delivering proactive monitoring would require rebuilding the core commercial model, not adding a feature** — Halodoc, Alodokter, and Good Doctor generate revenue when a user initiates a consultation session. The product team, engineering stack, clinical operations, and insurer-cashless billing are all designed around the episode trigger. A longitudinal CDM product requires: enrolled patient cohort management, proactive care coordinator outreach, lab order and result integration, medication titration workflows, and care plan documentation — none of which exists in the current product architecture. The build cost to add these capabilities exceeds an incremental feature cost; it requires a clinical infrastructure investment similar to building a GP clinic network. For a platform that claims breakeven (Alodokter) or is still burning capital (Halodoc post-$258M raised), that investment cannot be justified by the existing per-episode revenue model. (14, 15, 16)

- **RC2: Physician workforce on telemedicine platforms is structured for episodic availability, not longitudinal patient-doctor relationships — CDM requires a named GP, which the shift-based teleconsult model cannot provide** — Halodoc's 20,000+ medical practitioners are available for on-demand consultations in a shift-based queue model. The patient does not consistently see the same doctor. A CDM programme where a patient's HbA1c trend is reviewed quarterly, medication is adjusted based on cumulative history, and the GP knows the patient's social context (work stress, dietary habits, medication access) requires a named, ongoing doctor-patient relationship. The on-demand teleconsult model structurally prevents this: the doctor seeing a patient today has not seen their 2023 lab panel, does not know their current medication adherence, and cannot commit to a follow-up call in three months. The CDM model requires a different physician engagement structure — and a different billing structure to fund it. (16, 17)

- **RC3: Telemedicine platforms lack the physical clinical touchpoint infrastructure required for cardiometabolic CDM — blood pressure monitoring, HbA1c collection, and medication dispensing cannot be delivered fully through a video call** — Cardiometabolic CDM requires periodic laboratory testing (HbA1c quarterly, lipid panel annually), blood pressure monitoring at defined intervals, and medication dispensing with adherence tracking. Halodoc's e-pharmacy and Prodia's home collection service address parts of this, but there is no integrated system where a CDM-enrolled patient's home-collected HbA1c feeds into a care coordinator's dashboard, triggers a GP review call, and results in a medication titration that the pharmacist then dispenses. Each of these pieces exists in isolation across multiple platforms; no integration layer has been built. The structural absence of a physical GP anchor means that medication decisions requiring examination (checking for peripheral neuropathy, reviewing retinal screening result) cannot be made via teleconsult alone. (16, 19, 17)

- **RC4: Post-COVID de-prioritization of chronic disease in digital health funding — the 2021–2024 funding cycle rewarded MAU growth and GMV, not clinical outcome metrics, leaving CDM infrastructure unfunded** — Indonesia's digital health funding peaked in 2021–2022 and has slowed post-pandemic. The MAU metrics that drove Halodoc's $258M total raise and WhiteCoat's Good Doctor acquisition were user growth and GMV, not CDM enrolment rates or HbA1c improvement at 6 months. No Indonesian digital health platform has cited CDM clinical outcomes as a primary investor KPI. This is not a chance outcome: investors in the 2019–2023 cycle compared Indonesian digital health platforms to Ping An Good Doctor (China) and Halodoc's pitch was consumer acquisition, not longitudinal outcomes. CDM infrastructure — care coordinators, clinical protocol development, lab integration, physician training in chronic disease management — was not in the funded build plan. (14, 15, 18)

### Pain point 5.3

**Title:** Prodia's 354-outlet diagnostic network and MIKA's 4.15M private-pay outpatients represent the two largest existing patient touchpoints for CDM — but neither has built the GP-anchored longitudinal layer that converts screening or specialist encounters into enrolled management.

**Tests WH:** WH1 (supply-side gap is structural — SUPPORTED with specific mechanism: both diagnostic and specialist outpatient layers exist but are missing the GP coordination layer). Refined WH5 (not just insurers but providers don't deliver CDM — CONFIRMED as specific structural absence in the two most data-rich provider segments).

**Description:** Prodia Widyahusada (IDX: PRDA) operates 354 active diagnostic outlets in 80 cities and holds an estimated 39–40% revenue share of Indonesia's formal clinical laboratory market. Its B2C walk-in revenue (58% of Rp 2.25T in FY2024) means it sees hundreds of thousands of patients annually with metabolic panels, lipid profiles, and glucose readings that are the diagnostic substrate of CDM. Prodia Health Care (PHC) explicitly markets "Chronic Disease Management" as a service — yet PHC operates from a handful of flagship wellness clinics in Jakarta, Medan, and Surabaya; the CDM label is attached to a diagnostic-centric consultation, not a GP-anchored longitudinal programme; and PHC's patient count, CDM protocol adherence rates, and outcome data are absent from Prodia's FY2024 IDX filing. Mitra Keluarga (MIKA) sits at the opposite end of the spectrum: 85.2% private-pay, highest EBITDA margin in the sector (37.7%), 4.15M outpatient visits including specialist CDM-adjacent encounters (endocrinology, cardiology) — yet MIKA's outpatient model is specialist-centric and episodic. The diabetic patient seeing a MIKA endocrinologist quarterly is not enrolled in a care plan that coordinates labs, medications, lifestyle, and follow-through between specialist visits. The structural gap in both cases is identical: the diagnostic touchpoint (Prodia) and the specialist touchpoint (MIKA endocrinologist) both generate data relevant to CDM, but neither has a GP-as-longitudinal-anchor to convert that data into ongoing managed care.

**Who bears it:** Prodia walk-in patients who receive an abnormal HbA1c or lipid panel result and are directed to "consult a specialist" with no follow-up coordination. MIKA outpatients who see an endocrinologist quarterly but have no care coordinator managing adherence, lifestyle, or medication adjustments between specialist visits.

**Estimated cost:**

- **Prodia MCU/lab market — unmonetised CDM follow-up gap:** Indonesia's health check-up market was $656.2M in 2024 (CAGR 9.9% to 2030). Prodia holds ~39% of clinical lab market revenue (~$54M of its $139M FY2024 revenue is corporate B2B; $81M is B2C walk-in). A conservative estimate: if 20% of Prodia's B2C walk-in patients (approximately 200,000+ individuals based on revenue/package price ratios) have an abnormal metabolic finding, and each one-time lab visit could be converted to an enrolled CDM membership at Rp 2–5M/month, the conversion of even 5% of that abnormal cohort represents 10,000 enrolled CDM patients worth Rp 20–50B/year in recurring revenue — currently unmonetised. (21, 22) [ASSUMED-3: 20% abnormal metabolic finding rate among Prodia B2C walk-in patients extrapolated from population prevalence data (diabetes 11.3%, pre-diabetes 39.2%, hypertension 37.7%); actual Prodia abnormal-result rate not disclosed in FY2024 AR. — to validate: request Prodia IR for any internal data on B2C abnormal result rate or PHC referral conversion from walk-in lab clients.]
- **MIKA specialist-outpatient CDM gap — follow-through cost:** MIKA's implied revenue per outpatient visit was Rp 387,700 FY2024 average (Rp 1.61T / 4.15M visits). A MIKA endocrinology patient receiving four visits per year contributes ~Rp 1.55M/year to MIKA outpatient revenue. The same patient enrolled in a GP-anchored CDM programme with four GP visits + quarterly labs + care coordinator = Rp 24–60M/year recurring membership. The CDM programme generates 15–38x the revenue of episodic endocrinology-only care. Yet MIKA does not offer it — because the GP layer, care coordinator infrastructure, and CDM protocol are absent from MIKA's service architecture. (3)

The cost of uncontrolled diabetes is $930/patient/year in direct medical cost, 2.2x the cost of controlled disease. Prodia and MIKA combined see tens of thousands of the patients generating these outcomes annually — without a coordinated CDM layer to shift the control rate. (10, 1)

**Willingness to pay:** [Data gap] No published figure on Prodia or MIKA WTP for a CDM programme build-out or MSO partnership to provide the GP-anchored layer. PHC's wellness clinic pricing is not publicly disclosed. **Frequency:**

- Prodia FY2024: 354 active outlets, 80 cities, 107 kabupaten; B2C revenue 58%; corporate 5%; PHC wellness clinics in a handful of cities with no disclosed enrolled-CDM patient count. (21)

**Root Causes**

- **RC1: Prodia's commercial architecture is built on diagnostic transaction, not clinical programme delivery — the PHC wellness label exists but the CDM infrastructure (GP workforce, care coordinator, protocol, enrolled-patient registry) has not been built** — Prodia is a clinical laboratory business that generates 58% of revenue from B2C walk-in diagnostic tests. The core competency is sample collection, analysis, and reporting. PHC is an adjacency investment in a handful of flagship cities; the CDM label in PHC's service menu is not supported by disclosed clinical infrastructure (no published GP headcount at PHC, no CDM protocol documentation, no enrolled-patient registry, no outcome reporting in FY2024 AR). Building a GP-anchored CDM programme requires clinical programme design, GP hiring and training, care coordinator workforce, EMR with longitudinal patient tracking, and a billing model for recurring membership — all outside Prodia's demonstrated operational competency. The competitive moat of Prodia (354 outlet network, sample processing volume, lab accreditation) does not translate into CDM programme delivery capability without a substantial build. (21, 24)

- **RC2: MIKA's specialist-centric outpatient model has no structural home for a GP care coordinator — inserting a GP layer would require redesigning the referral and billing architecture that currently routes patients directly to specialist clinics** — MIKA's 17 hospitals and clinics are organised around specialist departments (cardiology, endocrinology, gastroenterology). A patient with DM arrives at the endocrinology outpatient clinic directly; there is no GP triage or care coordination layer. Adding a GP-anchored CDM programme would require: (a) a new GP workforce (MIKA's specialist-dominated model has not historically recruited general practitioners at scale); (b) a CDM billing category that does not exist in MIKA's current fee schedule; (c) a care coordinator function between GP visits and specialist follow-up; (d) a patient registry linking multiple specialist episodes into a longitudinal record. These four elements are operationally absent from MIKA's current structure. The investment required conflicts with MIKA's current capital allocation toward two new hospital openings in 2025 and capex-intensive surgical service expansion. (3, 12)

- **RC3: Both Prodia and MIKA depend on the same fee-for-service mechanism that prevents CDM investment — each abnormal lab result generates a revenue event (a new test, a specialist referral) rather than a care coordination obligation** — When a Prodia B2C walk-in patient receives an HbA1c of 7.5%, the Prodia encounter is complete: the report is delivered, the doctor consultation (if PHC) has billed for the visit. The patient's management going forward is their own responsibility or their specialist's. For MIKA, when the same patient presents to the endocrinologist, each visit is billed per-encounter. There is no structural incentive — no capitation, no outcome-linked payment, no care continuity obligation — that drives either provider to follow through with a longitudinal management plan. This is not negligence; it is the rational response to a fee-for-service environment where care coordination between episodes is unbilled. (5, 3, 21)

- **RC4: Neither Prodia nor MIKA has the physician workforce infrastructure to staff a GP-anchored CDM programme at the clinic level — the physician staffing model is specialist-heavy, not primary-care-GP-heavy** — Indonesia faces a structural GP scarcity in the private-pay market: the private sector GP model is dominated by BPJS-capitated clinics (Kimia Farma Klinik, independent GP practices) that operate at low-margin capitation rates and are not organized for preventive CDM. The affluent-facing private sector (Siloam, MIKA, Mayapada, RSPI) has built out specialist capacity — cardiologists, oncologists, neurosurgeons. There is no market precedent for a GP functioning as a longitudinal care anchor for an affluent Indonesian patient in a private hospital or diagnostic setting. The workforce, the role design, and the compensation structure for such a GP function do not currently exist in the private-pay sector at meaningful scale. ### Pain point 5.4

**Title:** Naluri's peer-reviewed CDM outcomes demonstrate the clinical model works in Indonesian populations — but its employer-funded, digital-only, Malaysia-headquartered architecture leaves the individual self-pay urban affluent slot structurally unserved, confirming WH5 and defining the MSO's entry point.

**Tests WH:** WH5 (insurers and providers not delivering CDM — CONFIRMED and refined: even the best-evidence CDM model in market structurally cannot serve the self-pay affluent slot). WH1 (affluent under-served — SUPPORTED: the clinical proof-of-concept exists but the commercial model cannot reach this population).

**Description:** Naluri (Naluri Hidup Sdn Bhd, Malaysia HQ, Indonesia operations) is the only entity in Indonesia's private health landscape with published, peer-reviewed evidence of effective CDM in an Indonesian employee population. Its retrospective observational study of 774 Indonesian employees (PMC, JMIR Medical Informatics, 2024) demonstrated significant HbA1c improvement (mean difference –0.14, p=0.008), total cholesterol reduction (–11.45 mg/dL, p<0.001), and HDL increase (+2.14 mg/dL, p<0.001) in the active coaching cohort versus control, over the 2021–2022 period. This is the only peer-reviewed evidence of a digitally-delivered CDM intervention producing measurable cardiometabolic outcomes in an Indonesian cohort. The structural limitation is threefold: (a) Naluri operates exclusively through employer-purchased benefit — BNI launched November 2024, H&M Group Production Office, mining and financial services corporate clients; there is no individual enrollment pathway for the self-pay urban affluent patient; (b) Naluri is digital-only with no physical GP anchor in Indonesia — the 16-week CDMP is delivered by remote coaches (dietitian, fitness coach, medical advisor, psychologist), but chronic medication management, clinical examination, and specialist coordination require a physical clinical touchpoint that Naluri does not provide; (c) Naluri is Malaysia-incorporated and staffed — its Indonesia operations are an extension of the Malaysia HQ, not a locally-anchored provider network. The MSO entry point is precisely the gap Naluri defines by its model: an evidence-based CDM protocol (Naluri's clinical protocol, or equivalent), combined with a physical GP anchor (which Naluri lacks), delivered through an individual subscription that does not require employer intermediation. The combination that does not exist is: (GP anchor + CDM clinical protocol + individual membership + physical clinic touchpoint in Tier-1 and Tier-2 cities).

**Who bears it:** Urban affluent / MC2 individuals with diabetes, hypertension, metabolic syndrome, or high cardiometabolic risk who are not employed by a Naluri-contracted corporate but want access to a digitally-enabled, evidence-based CDM programme. Also: independent and self-employed professionals (lawyers, doctors, entrepreneurs) in Jakarta, Surabaya, Bandung — the highest-density segment of MC2 — who have no employer benefit channel for CDM.

**Root Causes**

- **RC1: The employer-funded CDM model requires a large corporate HR department as the distribution intermediary — this channel is structurally inaccessible to the self-employed, SME-employed, and individual-subscription customer who represents the MC2 affluent self-pay segment** — Naluri's commercial model requires: (a) a corporate HR or Benefits director who signs a master services agreement; (b) employees who receive the benefit as part of their compensation; (c) the employer paying the programme fee from its health and wellness budget. The urban affluent self-pay individual — a 45-year-old Jakarta lawyer, a family business owner, an independent consultant — has no employer intermediary. They cannot access Naluri because Naluri does not sell to individuals. Building an individual subscription channel requires a B2C product, B2C billing, consumer marketing, and a different clinical services model (since individual users cannot be mandated to complete screenings or assessments as an employer might require). Naluri has not launched a B2C individual product in Indonesia as of the research date. (27, 28, 29)

- **RC2: Digital-only CDM without a GP anchor cannot manage medication titration for insulin-dependent or multi-drug cardiometabolic patients — the regulatory and clinical requirements for medication management require a physical clinical encounter** — Naluri's 16-week CDMP is behavioural coaching: dietitian, fitness coach, medical advisor (teleconsult), psychologist. It demonstrates outcome improvement for lifestyle-responsive metabolic markers. However, an HT patient on three antihypertensives whose BP is not controlled at three months needs a medication switch or titration — which requires: (a) a physical examination to rule out secondary causes; (b) a licensed physician to prescribe the new regimen; (c) dispensing through a pharmacy. Naluri's remote coaching team is not structured to make and execute medication decisions in Indonesia's regulatory environment. An MSO with a GP anchor bridges this gap — the GP sees the patient, adjusts the prescription, and the Naluri-style coaching protocol supports adherence in between. Without the GP, Naluri can improve lifestyle metrics but cannot fully manage pharmacologically complex patients. (27, 19)

- **RC3: Naluri's Malaysia headquarters and remote clinical staff create a structural disadvantage in navigating Indonesian clinical pathways, regulatory requirements (SatuSehat, BPJS PCare, KKI licensing), and physical lab-to-GP coordination** — Indonesian healthcare regulatory compliance for a CDM programme involves: SatuSehat data reporting, Konsil Kedokteran Indonesia (KKI) physician licensing compliance, BPJS PCare integration for the BPJS-registered subset of patients, and Indonesian pharmaceutical dispensing regulations. Naluri is Malaysia-incorporated; its medical advisors are operating under Malaysian regulatory frameworks and adapting to Indonesian compliance. An Indonesia-native GP-anchored MSO with local KKI-licensed GPs, SatuSehat-integrated EMR, and Indonesian pharmacy partnerships has a structural compliance advantage. (19, 24)

- **RC4: No commercial mechanism in Indonesia currently allows a CDM provider to receive risk-sharing or outcomes-based payment for self-pay individuals — the absence of an insurer-linked CDM benefit forces the model into pure self-pay subscription, limiting addressable market to the price-insensitive affluent tier** — The ideal CDM financing model would have an insurer pay a per-enrolled CDM member fee from the premium pool, based on demonstrated hospitalisation reduction. This is how mature CDM markets (UK QALY-based commissioning, Singapore CDMP) function. In Indonesia, no private insurer has a CDM benefit that pays a provider for outcomes (see Stage 4 pain points — insurers' wellness programmes are engagement-driven, not clinical-outcome-linked). The absence of insurer-linked CDM payment means the self-pay CDM model must recover all costs from the patient's OOP subscription fee — which limits the addressable market to the affluent self-pay tier willing and able to pay Rp 2–5M/month without insurer subsidy. This is simultaneously a market constraint and the MSO's specific entry point: the affluent segment is large enough and price-insensitive enough for a self-pay CDM membership to achieve commercial viability before insurer channel opens. ### New pain points (not in original hypotheses)

**New finding 5A: The Prolanis BPJS CDM programme's structural failure after 10 years (achieving only 4.1% of its chronic disease control target) creates a policy demand for a private-sector CDM model that works — the MSO's approach could be the demonstration case that triggers insurer and government uptake.**

This is not a pain point for the supply-side provider layer in the traditional sense — it is a structural opening. Prolanis' 2014 launch was Indonesia's only nationally mandated CDM programme. After 38 peer-reviewed studies and a decade of implementation, it achieves 4.1% of its chronic disease control performance target, with five identified failure categories (infrastructure/staffing, low coverage/participation/adherence, socioeconomic/cost barriers, cultural/health literacy barriers, pandemic disruption). ThinkWell's 2025 KBK policy brief explicitly calls for "redesigning the capitation system as a reward-based system with differentiated, achievable targets aligned with the government's massive Integrated Primary Healthcare (ILP) reform." The ILP reform is underway. A private-sector GP-anchored CDM platform that demonstrates measurable HbA1c control rates at scale would be the empirical counter-case to Prolanis's failure — and the model most likely to attract IFG Life / Mandiri Inhealth managed-care ambitions (see Stage 4), insurer CDM benefit structuring, and potentially government-funded pilot replication. This is a commercial and policy tailwind the MSO is positioned to capture if it publishes outcome data from Year 1–2 operations.
