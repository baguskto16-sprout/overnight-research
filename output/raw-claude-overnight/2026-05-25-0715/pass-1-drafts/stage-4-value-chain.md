## Stage 4: Major Insurer Products & Preventive/CDM Programmes

*Drafted: 2026-05-25 | Run ID: 2026-05-25-0715*
*Exchange rate: Rp 16,200/USD (May 2026 BI mid-rate)*

---

### Stage Definition & Boundaries

This stage documents the specific product architectures, named preventive and CDM programmes, provider-network partnerships, and digital integration capabilities of Indonesia's top commercial health insurers. In-scope: individual and group health insurance products, named wellness/preventive programmes (e.g., AIA Vitality, PRUWell), and any structured chronic disease management programme marketed to employer or individual clients. Out-of-scope: BPJS Kesehatan (referenced only where it defines the floor). Geography: Indonesia Tier-1 (Jabodetabek, Surabaya, Bandung) and Tier-2 cities; national GWP figures used where city-level data does not exist.

---

### Activities

1. **Design and price standard health product portfolios** — write and file RIPLAY (product disclosure documents) with OJK; set premium tariffs by age band, benefit class, and distribution channel; update annually in response to loss ratio experience.
2. **Administer cashless hospital networks** — contract private hospitals (panel hospitals); manage pre-authorisation, cashless billing, and fraud control; PRUPriority Hospital, AIA hospital network, and Allianz hospital panels are the three largest in the private individual-plan market.
3. **Operate wellness/behaviour-change programmes** — AIA Vitality (Indonesia-specific, app-based, launched January 2021), PRUWell (Prudential, launched May 2024), and IFG Life/Mandiri Inhealth "One by IFG" wellbeing app (launched December 2024) are the three named operational programmes; no insurer has a structured CDM longitudinal programme in market as a core insured benefit.
4. **Distribute through bancassurance, agency, broker, and digital channels** — individual policies via tied agents and exclusive bancassurance (AIA–BCA, AXA–Bank Mandiri); group policies via corporate brokers (Lockton, Aon, Willis Towers Watson Indonesia); digital MGA and comparison platforms (Lifepal, Qoala) for price-sensitive mass-market segments.
5. **Manage claims and underwriting repricing in response to loss ratio cycles** — all major insurers underwent material premium repricing in 2024 following 105–140% individual health loss ratios (AAJI); OJK POJK 36/2025 mandated co-payment (5% per claim) and reduced chronic illness waiting period from 12 to 6 months.

---

### Revenue Model

GWP from individual policyholders (monthly/annual) and employer groups (annual per-head premium); supplementary investment income on float; TPA administration fees from self-funded employer schemes; ancillary revenue from riders (outpatient, dental, critical illness add-ons) attached to base inpatient plans.

---

### Cost Drivers

Medical claims payments (dominant: AAJI reported health claims Rp 24.18 trillion in 2024 across life insurance sector); agent/broker commission (10–20% of individual premium first year); bancassurance distribution fee to bank partner; medical cost inflation at 13.6% CAGR (MMB 2025); hospital price inflation amplified by KRIS room standardization; wellness programme operating costs (AIA Vitality app infrastructure, point-reward redemption costs, health check-up partner subsidies).

### Section 5: Structural Gaps in Execution

1. **No insurer owns or operates a GP clinic chain.** All major insurers rely on panel hospital and clinic networks via fee-for-service cashless arrangements. No insurer has vertically integrated a primary care clinic network as part of its product ecosystem. This leaves the GP/primary care layer—the entry point for preventive and CDM services—entirely outside the insurer's operational control.

2. **No insurer has a dedicated longitudinal CDM pathway as an insured product benefit.** Prudential PRUWell, AIA Vitality, and IFG One by IFG are wellness/incentive programmes, not CDM pathways. Structured DM/HTN management (quarterly labs, medication titration, care coordinator, digital monitoring) is absent from every domestic insurer's product catalogue.

3. **Cashless claims systems are pre-authorisation workflows, not real-time API integrations.** The absence of bi-directional EMR integration means insurers cannot track clinical outcomes (HbA1c, BP control) for their insured chronically ill population even if they wanted to structure CDM programmes.

4. **Wellness programmes are premium management tools, not clinical interventions.** AIA Vitality's 25% premium discount and PRUWell's 20% discount are priced at the product level to attract healthy-at-sign-up policyholders (adverse selection mitigation), not to change clinical outcomes in the insured population.

5. **Group health contracts (1-year renewal) create a structurally insufficient time horizon for CDM investment return.** IFG Life acknowledged this after the Mandiri Inhealth acquisition, explicitly citing the need to move to "managed care" as a long-term cost containment strategy. No insurer has solved the mismatch between 1-year employer group contract cycles and the 3–7-year horizon needed to recoup preventive investment. (15)

6. **Digital broker platforms (Lifepal, Qoala, PasarPolis) surface product-price comparison but do not bundle preventive services.** The digital broker layer accelerates price-shopping behavior among health-sensitive consumers but does not enable preventive service delivery.

7. **Telehealth partnerships are referral agreements, not integrated care pathways.** The Halodoc–insurer partnerships (16 insurers listed) provide cashless telemedicine consultations for policyholders but do not create care coordination, specialist referral management, or CDM follow-through.

### Preliminary Pain Points (Stage 4 — Insurer Actor)

1. **Wellness programmes are structurally decoupled from clinical CDM outcomes** — AIA Vitality, PRUWell, and One by IFG reward healthy-behavior points and grant premium discounts but cannot identify, monitor, or manage the insurer's own chronically ill policyholders through a structured clinical pathway; the insurer has no visibility into whether a diabetic policyholder's HbA1c is controlled or deteriorating. (7), (8)

2. **PRUWell Medical's 20% premium discount for zero-claims policyholders rewards the already-healthy, not the chronically ill who most need active management** — the incentive model is adverse-selection mitigation dressed as wellness, not genuine health management for high-risk policyholders. (2)

3. **AIA Vitality's 30,000 active user count is trivially small relative to AIA Indonesia's individual policy book** — with AIA Indonesia's total premium income at ~Rp 10T (~$617M in 2023), even a conservative 0.5M individual policyholders would represent less than 6% Vitality programme penetration; the programme has not achieved scale needed to affect portfolio-level claims. [ASSUMED-14]

4. **IFG Life's acquisition of Mandiri Inhealth explicitly targets managed care as a cost containment strategy but has no product in market** — the December 2024 One by IFG app launch served ~5,000 IFG Group employees; the 1.8M Mandiri Inhealth group insurance participants have not received any new managed-care product. (12), (15)

5. **Hospital pre-authorisation workflows create friction in cashless claims that creates incentive for self-pay at retail clinics** — the 2–4 hour pre-auth time at panel hospitals drives insured patients to non-panel clinics for routine conditions, creating OOP expenditure for services that should be insured; this friction is especially acute for outpatient consultations. [ASSUMED-15]

6. **No insurer funds preventive screening (MCU, cancer screening, DM/HTN baseline labs) as a standard insured benefit** — PRUPriority Hospital "promotions" (mammography, thyroid screen) are discount arrangements from hospitals, not insured benefits; the insured individual bears the full screening cost OOP. (3), (4)

7. **Group health contract renewals are negotiated annually on claims performance, creating perverse incentive to under-invest in prevention** — an employer group that invests in CDM in year 1 and generates claims savings in year 3–5 will typically have switched insurers or renegotiated at lower premiums by year 2; the insurer cannot capture the return on preventive investment. (15)

8. **POJK 36/2025 co-payment (5% per claim, max Rp 3M per inpatient claim) increases financial friction at the point of care without reducing the structural absence of preventive benefits** — the regulation increases patient cost-sharing for curative claims but does not require or incentivize preventive benefit inclusion. (Stage 3 source 16)

9. **Rey Assurance's 50,000-life reach demonstrates proof-of-concept for preventive bundling but has not crossed the scale threshold where actuarial CDM claims data becomes meaningful** — at 50,000 lives and ~50% loss ratio, Rey has not yet demonstrated the model at 200,000+ lives where group-scale CDM savings become actuarially significant. (29)

10. **Bancassurance-dominant distribution (AIA–BCA, AXA–Bank Mandiri) creates channel conflict with preventive/CDM product designs that require clinical engagement rather than financial product framing** — bank staff are trained to sell premium and sum-assured, not health coaching pathways; this distribution architecture is misaligned with the engagement model needed for effective CDM. [ASSUMED-16]

11. **Employer group plans structurally exclude the informal-sector and self-employed MC2 household** — ~30–40% of urban MC2 (freelancers, SME owners) have no access to employer-sponsored group health and must purchase individual plans at retail premium post-43% repricing; they have no access to the managed-care tools that large-employer group plans can theoretically offer. (Stage 3 source AAJI H1 2024)

12. **Telehealth partnership depth is shallow — 16 insurers partnered with Halodoc creates commodity teleconsultation access but no differentiated CDM capability** — every insurer in the market now offers "teleconsultation access via Halodoc"; no insurer has a proprietary telemedicine capability with CDM protocol integration. (11)
