## Stage 5: Existing Preventive / CDM Delivery Landscape — Indonesia Tier-1 & Tier-2 Cities

*Drafted: 2026-05-25 | Run ID: 2026-05-25-0715*
*Exchange rate: Rp 16,200/USD (May 2026 BI mid-rate)*

---

### Stage Definition & Boundaries

This stage maps the competitive supply landscape for preventive care and chronic disease management (CDM) services in Indonesia's Tier-1 (Jabodetabek, Surabaya, Bandung) and Tier-2 (Medan, Makassar, Semarang, Palembang, Denpasar) cities. In-scope: private hospital outpatient services and MCU offerings; GP-clinic and primary-care chains; diagnostic and screening chains; digital-health and telemedicine platforms; specialized CDM and preventive startups; corporate wellness and EAP providers. Out-of-scope: BPJS Kesehatan-funded primary care (Puskesmas, PROLANIS) and public hospital outpatient, which define the floor the MSO would operate above.

### 1. Provider Landscape Map

#### 1.1 Private Hospital Groups

##### Siloam International Hospitals (IDX: SILO)

**Scale and revenue:** FY2024 net revenue Rp 12.2 trillion (~$753M), +9.1% YoY. Underlying EBITDA Rp 2.76 trillion (+3.2% YoY). Net profit Rp 950 billion (~$59M). (1)

**Network:** 41 hospitals across 23 provinces; 15 hospitals in Jabodetabek, 26 across Java, Sumatra, Kalimantan, Sulawesi, Bali, Nusa Tenggara, and Ambon. Additionally 73 Siloam Clinic units for broader catchment access. (1), (2)

**Volume:** FY2024 outpatient visits: 4.24 million (+7.5% YoY), consistently above 1 million visits per quarter; inpatient admissions 326,030 (+7.8% YoY); surgical procedures >100,000. (1)

**Revenue per outpatient visit:** Historical data shows Siloam's average revenue per outpatient visit at approximately Rp 1.2–1.5 million (~$74–$93) in 2021–2022; no current official per-visit figure disaggregated in 2024 press releases. [ASSUMED-1 — 2024 figure likely Rp 1.4–1.8M given +9.1% overall revenue growth over higher volume, but not confirmed in public disclosures.]

**Payer mix:** Private payers (OOP, corporate, insurance) ~82% of revenue; BPJS ~18%. (3)

**Service mix:** Predominantly acute inpatient and specialist outpatient. MCU services offered at multiple hospitals via the Siloam MCU package menu (executive, comprehensive, and basic packages ranging Rp 1.5M–Rp 8M+). CDM services exist through specialist outpatient clinics (endocrinology, cardiology) but are episodic/fee-for-service, not structured longitudinal programmes. (4)

**Insurer integrations:** Cashless panel for all major private insurers (Prudential, AIA, Allianz, AXA Mandiri, Manulife); AIA Altitude premium MCU at selected Siloam hospitals for top-tier AIA policyholders. BPJS cashless claims at selected hospitals.

**Positioning:** Mid-to-premium private; largest volume player; NGS (Next Generation Siloam) five-year plan targeting quality and capacity upgrade.

##### Mitra Keluarga (IDX: MIKA)

**Scale and revenue:** FY2024 revenue Rp 4.87 trillion (~$301M), +14.3% YoY. Net profit Rp 1.15 trillion (+25.1% YoY). EBITDA Rp 1.84 trillion (EBITDA margin 37.7%). (5)

**Network:** 17 hospitals and clinics in Jabodetabek and selected Java cities. Concentrated in Greater Jakarta (Bekasi, Depok, Kelapa Gading, Cibitung, Ciputra, Karawang, Tangerang). One Tier-2 presence (Surabaya). (5)

**Volume:** FY2024 outpatient visits: 4.154 million (+5.2% YoY); inpatient admissions: 720,000 (+6.4% YoY). Revenue per outpatient visit: Rp 387,700 IDR implied (Outpatient revenue Rp 1.612T / 4.154M visits) — consistent with reported 10.1% YoY increase in average revenue per outpatient visit in FY24 and Rp 587,000 per visit in Q1 2025 (the higher figure likely reflects MCU-heavy mix in Q1). (5), (6)

**Revenue breakdown:** Inpatient Rp 3.263 trillion (67%); Outpatient Rp 1.612 trillion (33%) in FY2024. (5)

**Payer mix:** Private patients (non-JKN) expanded to 85.2% of revenue in FY2024 (+3.6 percentage points YoY), reinforcing premium private-pay positioning. (5)

**Service mix:** General + specialist outpatient; MCU packages; no structured longitudinal CDM programme. Revenue driver is case intensity (complex cases, surgical), not preventive or CDM volume.

**Insurer integrations:** Cashless panel for top-5 private insurers. High proportion of corporate and insurance payers in private mix (breakdown not published).

**Positioning:** Premium Tier-1 city concentrated; highest EBITDA margin in the sector (37.7%); explicitly avoiding JKN volume expansion.

##### Medikaloka Hermina (IDX: HEAL)

**Scale and revenue:** FY2024 net revenue Rp 6.72 trillion (~$415M), +16.1% YoY. Net profit Rp 535.9 billion (+22.5% YoY). (7)

**Network:** 52 hospitals in 63 cities across 17 provinces as of December 2024. Largest hospital count among IDX-listed groups; strong Tier-2 and peripheral Tier-1 coverage (Cikarang, Depok, Bogor, Bekasi, Cibubur in Jabodetabek; Semarang, Makassar, Medan, Palembang in Tier-2). (7)

**Volume (FY2023 most recent detailed):** Outpatient visits 8.0 million (vs. 6.1 million in 2019); aggregate Bed Occupancy Rate 71% in 2023 rising toward 2024. (8)

**Payer mix:** JKN-heavy; BPJS accounts for approximately 73–79% of outpatient visits and 75–79% of inpatient days in recent quarters. Private payer mix ~21–27%. (8)

**Service mix:** Strong maternal and child health (mom-and-baby; perinatology; neonatal ICU); general outpatient with GP and specialist clinics; some MCU services. CDM offering extremely limited given BPJS-dominant patient mix; BPJS CDM pathways (PROLANIS) at affiliated Puskesmas level, not systematized at Hermina hospital level.

**Positioning:** Scale player in mass-market and lower-middle class segments; growing revenue via volume + KRIS tariff uplift; not an affluent-facing platform.

#### 1.5 Specialized CDM / Preventive Startups

##### Naluri (Malaysia HQ, Indonesia operations)

**Model:** Evidence-based digital therapeutics platform for corporate clients; delivers Chronic Disease Management Programme (CDMP) — a 16-week structured, intensive digital coaching programme targeting cardiometabolic risk factors (DM, HTN, hypercholesterolemia, obesity) and mental health. Operates via employer-purchased benefit; coaches are multidisciplinary (dietitian, fitness coach, medical advisor, psychologist). Indonesia operations deployed in mining, manufacturing, and financial services corporate clients. (25)

**Published outcomes (PMC peer-reviewed, 2024):** Retrospective analysis of 774 Indonesian employees (2021–2022 health screenings). Active coaching group showed significant improvement in HbA1c (mean difference –0.14, p=0.008), total cholesterol (–11.45 mg/dL, p<0.001), HDL (+2.14 mg/dL, p<0.001) vs. no-coaching group which deteriorated on multiple metrics. (25)

**CDM assessment:** Closest to a longitudinal CDM model in this landscape. However: (a) employer-funded, not individual-pay or insurer-reimbursed; (b) digital-only (no GP anchor, no physical clinical touchpoints in Indonesia for chronic medication management); (c) 16-week structured programme with no confirmed indefinite longitudinal follow-up model. Serves large corporate cohorts (mining, manufacturing), not the individual urban affluent self-pay segment. (25)

**Malaysia HQ dependency:** Naluri Hidup Sdn Bhd is Malaysia-registered; Indonesia operations are an expansion; clinical staff and infrastructure mostly remote/digital. Not GP-anchored at a physical clinic level.

**Positioning:** Best-in-class digital CDM coaching for employer populations; digital-only; corporate channel; not individual-membership; not GP-anchored in Indonesia.

**Finding:** No existing Tier-1 player operates a GP-anchored, asset-light, individually enrolled longitudinal CDM programme at the affluent price point in Indonesia. ### 3. GP-Anchored MSO Competitive Set

The following players are the closest structural analogues to a GP-anchored, asset-light, longitudinal preventive + CDM MSO:

1. **Klinik Pintar** — Closest in primary-care clinic management infrastructure and tech enablement; operates owned GP clinics in Greater Jakarta; has corporate health clinic operations; but does not run a longitudinal CDM programme and has not positioned toward individual-membership preventive care for affluent patients. Funding stage: Series A1, $5M (December 2023). (14)

2. **Naluri Indonesia** — Closest in CDM methodology (structured cardiometabolic management, peer-reviewed outcomes in Indonesian employees); but digital-only (no GP anchor); employer-funded (not individual-pay); Malaysia-incorporated. Not a market competitor in the MSO sense; potential partner in digital CDM protocol. (25)

3. **Prodia Health Care (PHC)** — Closest in declaring "Chronic Disease Management" as a service line; physically present in Tier-1 cities; lab-anchored wellness. Limitation: diagnostic company extending into wellness, not GP-anchored primary care; no confirmed enrolled-patient CDM model; membership fee structure not documented. (17)

4. **Rey Assurance** — Insurance subscription model bundling preventive care with curative coverage; 50,000+ lives reached; health insurtech, not a clinic operator. Adjacent, not direct competition in clinic-based MSO model. (Stage 4 source 29)

**Conclusion:** None of the above replicate the proposed MSO's specific combination — GP as the longitudinal anchor, individual membership subscription, physical clinic presence, ongoing medication and lab management, and affluent price point. The closest competitive gap: Klinik Pintar could pivot toward this model but has not done so; Naluri could provide the CDM protocol but lacks the physical GP layer.

### 6. White-Space Confirmation

The following features are absent from all existing Tier-1 players' current products and service designs in Indonesia:

1. **Membership-based individual subscription with annual fee that covers longitudinal GP-led CDM** — No existing player (hospital, diagnostic, digital health, or startup) offers an individual-pay annual membership that funds: (a) a named GP as longitudinal primary care provider, (b) quarterly cardiometabolic lab monitoring, (c) chronic medication management (titration, adherence tracking), and (d) care coordination to specialist as needed. Prodia PHC comes closest in brand intent but has no confirmed enrolled-membership model with these four components.

2. **GP as the anchor for ongoing preventive care, not episodic specialist referral** — All private hospital outpatient services in Indonesia deliver specialist-centric care; the GP layer is either absent (Siloam, MIKA, Mayapada) or BPJS-facing capitation (Hermina, Kimia Farma). The GP functioning as a longitudinal primary care anchor for affluent patients is not an established model.

3. **Structured cardiometabolic CDM pathway with clinical monitoring at the clinic level** — PROLANIS (BPJS government CDM programme) exists for JKN patients at Puskesmas level; private-sector equivalents do not exist. Naluri is digital-only and employer-funded. No private clinic chain runs enrolled cohort DM/HTN management with quarterly HbA1c/BP tracking and medication titration at a clinic visit level for self-pay affluent patients.

4. **Affluent price point for preventive services** — Current preventive offerings are either: (a) low-cost BPJS-subsidized (PROLANIS, annual health screening for JKN members), or (b) one-time transactional MCU packages (Rp 1.5M–Rp 8M+ per event). No player offers a recurring Rp 2–5M/month or Rp 20–50M/year membership model covering ongoing preventive management.

5. **Asset-light MSO structure enabling GP independence with shared services** — Existing hospital groups are asset-heavy (owned hospital buildings); Klinik Pintar provides software but not the clinical programme; no entity currently offers GP practices access to shared CDM protocols, lab procurement, IT/EMR, and chronic disease management pathways without requiring full hospital integration.

### Preliminary Pain Points (Stage 5 — Supply-Side Actors)

1. **All private hospital outpatient services are episodic and fee-for-service; no existing player has structured an enrolled CDM population with recurring revenue per managed patient.** The outpatient income model (per-visit, per-procedure) creates no incentive for the provider to prevent the next hospitalization — the hospital's revenue increases when the patient deteriorates and requires admission. (1), (5), (7)

2. **Prodia's PHC wellness clinics market "Chronic Disease Management" but have not disclosed enrolled patient count, CDM protocol adherence rates, or outcome data** — evidence of operational CDM at scale at PHC is absent from audited financial reports; the label exists, the programme may not. (15), (17) [ASSUMED-4]

3. **The MCU market is purely transactional — 31,753 MCU patients at Mayapada in FY2024 were not enrolled in any follow-up care pathway** — the MCU result generates a report, not a care plan; there is no GP who follows up on abnormal findings with a structured management plan. (9)

4. **Digital health platforms (Halodoc, Alodokter, Good Doctor) are structurally incapable of delivering longitudinal CDM** because their teleconsultation model is patient-initiated, per-episode; there is no proactive outreach to a chronically ill enrolled patient, no medication titration capability at scale, and no laboratory coordination. Bain survey data confirms Indonesian consumers in 2023 identified "lack of affordable, accessible care and long waiting times" as key chronic care management obstacles — a gap these platforms have not bridged. (20)

5. **Siloam's 4.24 million outpatient visits in 2024 include almost no systematic preventive CDM** — SILO's 82% private-pay mix means it has a high-value patient pool, but the episode-driven consultation model means Siloam has zero structural insight into how many of its outpatients have uncontrolled DM, HTN, or dyslipidemia, and no mechanism to proactively manage them between visits. (1)

6. **Mitra Keluarga's 85.2% private-pay patient concentration is the highest in the sector, representing the closest existing patient pool to the MSO's target segment, but MIKA has no CDM or preventive programme** — the affluent MIKA patient who gets specialist consultation is not followed longitudinally; if they have DM managed by an endocrinologist on a 3-monthly visit schedule, there is no GP coordinating labs, medications, diet, and lifestyle between those specialist visits. (5)

7. **Klinik Pintar's 22 owned clinics and 1,500+ software-partner clinics represent the largest GP-clinic infrastructure in urban Indonesia, but the CDM capability has not been built** — the software enables BPJS PROLANIS compliance reporting but not a private-pay enrolled CDM programme. (14)

8. **Naluri's published clinical outcomes (HbA1c improvement, cholesterol reduction in Indonesian employees) are the only peer-reviewed evidence of effective CDM in Indonesia's private sector** — and Naluri cannot serve the individual self-pay affluent population because its delivery model requires employer mandating and funding; there is no individual enrollment pathway. (25)

9. **Prodia's 354-outlet network provides geographic access that would be required for a diagnostic-integrated CDM model, but Prodia has not built the GP layer** — PHC's wellness clinics are in a handful of cities; the remaining 330+ outlets are pure diagnostic labs; this creates a potential partnership opportunity but not a standalone CDM competitor.

10. **Good Doctor / WhiteCoat's 2,500 corporate clients and 7,500 combined corporate partners represent a large employer-contracted population, but none of these contracts fund longitudinal CDM at the GP level** — the employer-telemedicine model provides episodic consultation; it does not deliver medication titration, specialist referral coordination, or annual care plan revision for cardiometabolic patients. (22)

11. **SehatQ, Klikdokter, and smaller digital platforms add to the provider count without adding CDM capability** — the telemedicine market is fragmented (Indonesia held 26.4% of SEA telehealth market in 2024) but all players are structurally episodic. More platform options for acute-episodic care does not reduce the white space in longitudinal CDM. (22)

12. **The dual-speed market identified by L.E.K. Consulting** — burgeoning mass segment (BPJS-served) vs. growing affluent segment demanding cutting-edge services — has produced investment primarily in acute specialty capacity (cardiac surgery, oncology, robotic surgery at premium hospitals) rather than in preventive or CDM infrastructure that would reduce long-term acute demand. (29)

13. **Customer acquisition for preventive care lacks an individual-membership channel** — current acquisition is corporate-contracted (Good Doctor, Naluri, Klinik Pintar corporate clinics) or episodic-individual (hospital walk-in, Prodia lab walk-in). There is no subscription or membership product in the private market through which an affluent individual can pay a recurring fee to be managed preventively, even though Bain's 2023 survey found 91% of APAC consumers prefer a single touchpoint for managing their health. (19)
