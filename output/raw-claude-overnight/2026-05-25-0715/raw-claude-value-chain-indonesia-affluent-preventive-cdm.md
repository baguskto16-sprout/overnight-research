**Topic:** Sizing the addressable urban affluent and upper-middle population for an asset-light, GP-anchored MSO delivering preventive care + chronic disease management, funded via OOP, private insurance, or employer programmes.

## Executive summary

**Addressable consumer base — Tier-1 Indonesia, May 2026:**

- **Greater Jakarta (Jabodetabek) Middle Class (MC):** ~12 million people (BPS Susenas March 2024, refined from World Bank 2019 Aspiring Indonesia data; national MC 47.85M, Jabodetabek share 25%).
- **Combined Tier-1 metro MC (Jabodetabek + Surabaya + Bandung + Medan + Semarang and other Tier-1 metros):** ~24 million people.
- **National Upper Class (UC):** ~1.29 million (BPS Susenas; Susenas systematically under-counts wealthy households, so floor not ceiling).
- **Discretionary preventive/CDM spending threshold:** Rp 5,000,000/month per-capita (~USD 309/month at Rp 16,200/USD) — entry point to BPS MC2 band where food drops below 1/3 of consumption and a Rp 300,000–500,000/month MSO membership (USD 18–31) is absorb-able without financial hardship.

**Total addressable healthcare spend (national, all payers, 2023):**

- **Total Health Expenditure (THE):** Rp 614.5 trillion / USD 38B (WHO Indonesia NHA 2023).
- **OOP share:** 28.6% of THE.
- **Private health insurance GWP 2024:** Rp 40.19 trillion / USD 2.48B (+53% YoY) with 31.34M policies (OJK via ANTARA News, June 2025).
- **Group health insurance GWP 2024 combined:** AAUI Q4 2024 (general insurance, mostly group): Rp 11.82T / USD 743M; plus AAJI life-sector group health estimate ~Rp 12.5T / USD 785M = **~USD 1.53B combined** (AAUI Q4 2024 official PDF; AAJI 2024 full-year statement).

**Medical cost inflation 2025:** 19% (Mercer Marsh Benefits Health Trends 2025 — highest in Asia outside China). Indonesia 2022 medical trend: 12.3%; 2023: 13.6% (MMB Global Medical Trends Survey, firm-imprint primary). 5–6x general CPI of 2.6–5.5%.

**Structural conclusion:** The addressable affluent + upper-middle base in Tier-1 Indonesia is materially large (~24M MC + 1M+ UC); they spend at private rates; insurance funds inpatient but not preventive/CDM; no insurer or provider operates a longitudinal CDM platform at scale; employer benefit budgets are breached by 19% medical trend. The MSO white-space is real.

## Stage 1 — Affluent / upper-middle population sizing

### Definition and boundaries

BPS (Badan Pusat Statistik) classifies Indonesian households into five welfare bands using a 3.5x–17x national poverty line cutoff (World Bank "Aspiring Indonesia" 2019 methodology, adopted by BPS in 2024):

- **Upper Class (UC):** >17x national poverty line (~Rp 9.6M/month per-capita)
- **Middle Class (MC):** 3.5x–17x national poverty line
  - **MC2 (upper-middle):** ~Rp 5.0M–9.6M/month per-capita
  - **MC1 (lower-middle):** ~Rp 2.0M–5.0M/month per-capita
- **Aspiring Middle Class (AMC):** 1.5x–3.5x poverty line
- **Vulnerable / Poor:** ≤1.5x poverty line

### Demographic composition

- Age structure (UC + MC2 in Tier-1): median age 38–42; high adult-CDM exposure (hypertension prevalence rises sharply 35+).
- Employment: **40.89% formal-sector workers** (BPS Sakernas August 2023 — official headline; informal sector ~59.11% nationally, ~40–48% urban Tier-1). ### Disease prevalence relevant to CDM (Indonesia adult)

- **Hypertension:** 30.8% (Riskesdas / SKI 2023 national); 78.87% of hypertensive adults are uncontrolled (Alfian et al. *Hypertension Research* 2026 from 2023 national survey).

- **Indonesia all-payer diabetes cost:** USD 308 per person per year (~Rp 4.9M, IDF Atlas 11th ed). Private rate 3–5x.

### Aspirational vs discretionary threshold

**Rp 5,000,000/month per-capita (USD 309/month)** is the consumption threshold at which preventive/CDM spend shifts from aspirational to discretionary regular behaviour:
- Below this threshold (MC1, AMC): food remains >40% of consumption; preventive spending is one-off and event-driven.
- At and above (MC2, UC): food drops below 1/3; discretionary categories grow; ~Rp 228K/month already spent on health; absorbing a Rp 300–500K/month CDM-MSO membership is feasible.

Source: Susenas household expenditure share by welfare class (BPS); cross-validated by IFLS-5 (Indonesia Family Life Survey, RAND).

### Named operational preventive/CDM programmes (insurer-side)

| Insurer | Programme | Launch | Reach | Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AIA Indonesia | **AIA Vitality** | 2021 | ~30,000 active users (mid-2023; 2024 not disclosed) | Behavioural incentive app (footsteps, exercise minutes, biometric screening) |
| Prudential | **PRUWell** | May 2024 | Not disclosed | Premium discount up to 20% for zero-claim policyholders; hospital screening promotion |
| IFG Life | **One by IFG** | December 2024 | Internal IFG Group employees (~5,000) | EAP/wellness app — not marketed to Mandiri Inhealth's 890K participants |

**Key finding:** None of these is a true longitudinal CDM product. AIA Vitality is a wellness gamification app — points for biometric improvement, not enrolled clinical management. PRUWell is an actuarial discount mechanism — rewards healthy policyholders, doesn't manage at-risk ones. One by IFG is an internal EAP. **No commercial insurer in Indonesia operates a structured longitudinal CDM programme (diagnosed-DM/HTN/dyslipidaemia enrolled care pathway with HbA1c/BP outcome accountability) as either an insured benefit or a value-add service at meaningful scale.**

### Failure-mode pattern (cross-insurer)

1. **Wellness programmes are adverse-selection tools, not health management** — AIA Vitality at ~30K active users out of ~900K-1M individual policyholders means <5% engagement; PRUWell 20% discount filters for healthy, not at-risk.
2. **1-year group contract horizon misaligns with CDM ROI** — Indonesian peer-reviewed literature shows CDM programmes return $2.83–$9.89 per dollar invested over 2+ year horizons (PMC8582197); single-year contracts make insurer-funded CDM uneconomic.
3. **No clinical-data infrastructure** — claims systems are pre-authorisation workflows. SATU SEHAT national EMR adoption was 57% complete by October 2024 [tagged ASSUMED on specific %]; AdMedika's Avatar AI does fraud audit not population management.
4. **No insurer owns GP-led primary care** — provider networks are hospital-centric (Siloam, MIKA, HEAL, RS Premier, RS Pondok Indah); 51% of patients self-route to hospitals over primary care.
5. **POJK 36/2025 (December 2025)** — OJK regulation explicitly encouraging managed care models; 1-year compliance window creates procurement opening for MSO contracting.

## Cross-stage observations

### Confirmed working hypotheses

- **WH1 (Affluent under-served by structured preventive/CDM)** — **CONFIRMED**. Stage 4 (no insurer CDM programme) + Stage 5 (no provider CDM at scale) jointly establish the supply gap. Stage 2 establishes spending capacity (Rp 5M/month threshold + USD 63–83 Q5 OOP).
- **WH2 (Discretionary threshold at upper-middle income)** — **CONFIRMED**. Rp 5,000,000/month per-capita threshold identified; consumption shift documented; CDM-MSO membership at Rp 300–500K/month feasible above threshold.
- **WH3 (Insurance is inpatient-oriented)** — **CONFIRMED**. Stage 3 cross-insurer coverage matrix shows uniform inpatient-led structure; outpatient sub-limited; CDM excluded. AAUI/AAJI loss ratios driven primarily by inpatient claims.
- **WH4 (Employer is dominant access route for upper-middle)** — **CONFIRMED**. AAJI 2024: 95M group-insured lives vs 18M individual. Stage 6 documents employer health spend USD 3.0–3.5B. Formal sector ~40.89% (BPS Sakernas Aug 2023) — half of working-age MC are group-eligible.

### Working hypothesis to REFINE

- **WH5 (Insurers piloting structured preventive/CDM but execution fragmented)** — **REFINED TO REJECTED**. Stage 4 finding is stronger than the original hypothesis: insurers have wellness *marketing* (Vitality, PRUWell, One by IFG) but **no insurer operates a structured CDM programme as an insured benefit or scaled service**. The gap is not "fragmented execution" — it is "zero execution." This refines the MSO entry thesis: the MSO is not partnering with insurers to integrate CDM pilots, it is becoming the CDM execution layer that insurers structurally cannot build (1-year contract horizon, no clinical-data infrastructure, no GP-network ownership).
