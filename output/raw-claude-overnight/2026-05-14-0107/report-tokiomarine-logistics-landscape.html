Executive Summary

The structural authorization gap at the sub-carrier tier is the single largest unaddressed risk-management opportunity in worldwide freight.

Physical cargo theft, USA 2025
$725M

+60% year-over-year. Per-incident average loss rose from $187K (2023) to $274K (2025), a 46% increase in two years (CargoNet, FBI IC3).

Cargo InsurTech reinsurance capacity
−50%

Munich Re and Swiss Re cut MGA exposure by more than half in 2024 after the $4B Vesttoo fraud — tightening capacity precisely as cargo loss frequency accelerates.

Findings

  1. RTTVPs (project44, FourKites, Shippeo, Wakeo) lose granular signal at the sub-carrier handoff. project44 markets 245,000+ connected carriers, yet integration depth collapses once a 3PL subcontracts to non-integrated regional carriers — the layer where 53–69% of journeys actually move.
  2. Telematics is ubiquitous; insurance pricing has not caught up. 88% of fleets deploy telematics (SambaSafety 2025, n=402), but only 23% of insurer programs are underwriting-led. Premiums still price off declared values and pooled actuarial tables, not journey signals.
  3. Incident response is reactive and uncoordinated. No common workflow links shipper, 3PL, sub-carrier, insurer, and law enforcement; coordination begins manually by phone and email. Claims platforms shorten payout but rarely shorten time-to-response.
  4. Worldwide solutions exist in every stage except the sub-carrier authorization layer. 568 evidence points across 328 unique source domains map 60+ named vendors — none verify operator legitimacy, insurance, and safety record at the sub-carrier tier with insurer-grade data discipline.
The ask A Wave-1 venture sprint with Tokio Marine + ID&E to validate the sub-carrier authorization product — eight weeks of interviews with marine cargo underwriters and Indonesia / Thailand 3PLs, against a $2M deck-ready opportunity sizing.

Analytical framework

The freight journey was sliced into six actor stages, scanned worldwide, and mapped against SEA deployment readiness.

Solutions were grouped by where they operate in the journey, not by product category. A vendor selling "TMS" with embedded carrier vetting was tagged Stage 2 if its risk-management value lay in dispatch, Stage 1 if it lay in onboarding. This lets the gap analysis be honest about which journey moments are crowded with vendors and which are under-served.

Stage 1 — Vendor Procurement

Who can be trusted to carry the cargo?
Carrier vetting marketplaces, KYB layers, identity / fraud detection, insurance verification. The earliest point of risk leverage.

Stage 2 — TMS & Dispatch

How does the load get tendered and accepted?
Enterprise / 3PL / carrier TMS, digital marketplaces, procurement software, control-tower-lite. Booking-layer fraud lives here.

Stage 3 — Fleet & Telematics

What's the carrier's operational signal?
Samsara, Motive, Geotab, Verizon Connect, Lytx, Netradyne. Rich data — locked inside the carrier's own SaaS contract.

Stage 4 — In-Transit Visibility

Where is the load right now, and is it OK?
project44, FourKites, Shippeo, Wakeo, Descartes MacroPoint, cargo IoT (Tive, Roambee, Controlant). Signal-loss at the sub-carrier handoff.

Stage 5 — Security & Escort

Who protects high-value or high-risk loads?
CargoNet, Overhaul GSOC, Solutions Group International, secure-parking networks, escort/convoy providers. Concentrated in NA/EU; thin in SEA.

Stage 6 — Cargo Insurance & Claims

Who absorbs the loss, and how fast?
Tokio Marine, Zurich, Chubb, AXA XL; Marsh, Aon; Loadsure, Breeze, Parsyl, Cover Genius. Pricing still anchored to declared values.
What the framework excludes — by design Last-mile parcel delivery (different unit economics), warehouse-internal WMS, port-side maritime systems, pure spot-pricing marketplaces with no risk dimension, passenger telematics. Sources: see the full out-of-scope tag in the run input artifact.

Stage 1 of 6

Vendor procurement and counterparty risk is the earliest, highest-leverage moment — and per-incident loss is up 46% in two years.

Primary user
Shippers, 3PLs, freight brokers
Business models
SaaS seat, per-carrier verification fee
SEA presence
Thin — most NA-centric, no SEA office

Representative vendors

Highway
Carrier vetting marketplace · USA · private
What it does
Identity-verifies carriers in real time at booking; blocks stolen-identity dispatches.
Trade-off
NA-dispatch native; no SEA carrier coverage.
RMIS
Onboarding + monitoring · USA · acquired by DAT
What it does
Continuous insurance / authority / safety monitoring of carriers in a broker's roster.
Trade-off
Pulls FMCSA / SAFER data — US-only by construction.
Carrier411 / MyCarrierPortal
Vetting feeds · USA · private
What it does
Aggregated freight-fraud watchlists, "FraudGuard" alerts, broker-shared blacklists.
Trade-off
Watchlists are reactive; identity-spoofing outruns them.
The headline pain — quantified Per-incident average loss climbed $187,895 (2023) → $202,364 (2024) → $273,990 (2025), a 46% rise in two years. Aggregate US cargo theft losses jumped from $454.9M (2024) to $725M (2025), a 60% YoY increase. TIA-surveyed companies report average annual fraud losses above $400,000; 22% lost over $200,000 in a single six-month period (1, 2, 3, 4).

Connected operations · USA · public (IOT)
What it does
Vehicle + driver + asset telematics on a single SaaS, sold to fleet operators. Real-time GPS, video, fuel, hours-of-service.
Trade-off

Motive (KeepTruckin)
AI-first fleet platform · USA · pre-IPO
What it does
ELD-anchored telematics with AI-coached safety and embedded factoring / fuel cards.
Trade-off

Nirvana Insurance
Insurer-native telematics underwriter · USA · private
What it does
Underwrites commercial trucking using telematics-fed risk scores rather than declared values.
Trade-off
Series D figures conflict ($100M vs $80M; $1.5B vs $830M valuation, Form D to confirm).
The headline pain When a 3PL subcontracts a leg to a small or mid-size carrier running Samsara, Motive, or a regional telematics provider, the telematics signal generated during that leg is structurally owned and contractually gatekept by the carrier — not the shipper, not the 3PL, not the insurer (Stage 3 PP-3.1). The data exists; the parties who underwrite the risk cannot see it.

Prioritization

Wave 1 enters at the sub-carrier authorization gap — the only journey moment with both real money lost and no productized incumbent.

Each wave targets a journey moment where Tokio Marine + ID&E have an asymmetric right-to-win: regulatory standing in SEA, balance-sheet capacity, and an existing cargo-underwriting franchise. Sequencing is set by where competitive moat builds fastest — not where vendor activity is heaviest.

Wave 1 · Months 1–4
Sub-carrier authorization
Productized vetting + insurance verification + safety scoring at the sub-carrier tier. Insurer-grade data discipline absent in Stage 1 incumbents. Pilot lanes in Indonesia, Thailand.
Wave 2 · Months 5–9
Telematics-fed underwriting
Pricing model that ingests Wave 1 sub-carrier scores + Stage 3 telematics consents. Closes the 23% insurer-led underwriting gap. SambaSafety baseline (US) as upper bound; SEA pilot pricing in cooperation with TM Indonesia.
Wave 3 · Months 10–14
Incident-response control tower
Stage 5 five-party workflow productized: shipper / 3PL / sub-carrier / insurer / law-enforcement workflow. Capitalizes on Wave 1+2 data foundation.
Why this sequence and not a Stage 4 RTTVP play Stage 4 is saturated with $1B+ funded vendors (project44, FourKites, Shippeo) and the recent E2open acquisition consolidates the category. Wave 1 enters at a journey moment where the largest incumbents (RMIS, Highway, Carrier411) have no insurer-grade product and no SEA presence — a defensible position TM + ID&E can build into quickly.
