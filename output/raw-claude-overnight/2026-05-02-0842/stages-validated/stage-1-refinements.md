## Refinement 1 — VC-1.7 (PKS supply Indonesia + Malaysia)

**Original:** Indonesia + Malaysia combined PKS availability is greater than 18 million tonnes per year. *(single source: Verified Market Reports)*

**Refined:** Indonesia and Malaysia combined generate an estimated **15–19 million tonnes of PKS per year** (central estimate 16–17 Mt at a 5–5.5% PKS/FFB yield ratio; the >18 Mt upper end requires the full 6% engineering yield). Bottom-up: Indonesia CPO 2023 = 50.07 Mt (GAPKI 2024); Malaysia CPO 2023 = 18.55 Mt with OER 19.86% (MPOB 2023); FFB ≈ 228 Mt (Indonesia) + 93 Mt (Malaysia); apply 5–6% PKS/FFB yield. APCASI/GAPKI 2022 cited Indonesia PKS 2019 = 9.97 Mt directly, implying ~4.6% effective yield (lower-bound floor).

## Refinement 2 — VC-1.13 (Thailand industrial coal)

**Original:** Thailand industrial coal consumption ~11.2 Mt (2023), all imported. *(single source: Statista secondary)*

**Refined:** Thailand's industrial sector (non-power manufacturing) consumed **11.21 Mt of imported coal in 2023** per EPPO Table 4.1-2 (preliminary). Total Thailand coal imports were 18.06 Mt (100% imported — Thailand has no commercial coal mining), with an additional 12.74 Mt of domestic lignite consumed exclusively by EGAT's Mae Moh power station. Of the 18.1 Mt imported coal, ~11.72 Mt was sub-bituminous originating predominantly from Indonesia; ~6.00 Mt was bituminous from Australia/Russia. Power-sector coal totaled 6.84 Mt (4.06 Mt IPP + 2.78 Mt SPP); SPP includes industrial co-gen, so a broader industrial framing puts industrial coal at up to ~14 Mt.

## Refinement 3 — PP1.1-6 (METI biomass FIT halt)

**Original:** METI announced (~Feb 2025) that it will halt acceptance of new biomass FIT projects from FY2026 onward. *(secondary/think-tank only)*

**Refined:** Japan's METI formally announced on **21 March 2025** (following its Procurement Price Calculation Committee decision of 17 January 2025) that **large-scale general woody biomass power generation (≥10 MW / 10,000 kW)** and **all liquid biomass power generation (all scales)** will not be covered by FIT or FIP schemes from FY2026 onward. Sub-10 MW biomass projects remain supported at ¥24/kWh. Because the operational Japanese plants driving Vietnam wood pellet and Indonesia PKS imports are predominantly ≥10 MW, this policy ends the subsidy pipeline for new large-scale imported-fuel biomass demand — validating the "demand-cliff" framing for the new-build pipeline (existing plants on legacy FIT contracts continue to consume imports for their 20-year terms). METI also reduced the auction tender ceiling for existing large biomass from 120 MW to 30 MW.

- (R3.2) Japan Energy Hub coverage of Jan 2025 committee decision — https://japanenergyhub.com/news/large-biomass-fit-fip-discontinuation/

## Refinement 4 — PP1.1-11 (Japan FIT biomass rate)

**Original:** Japan FIT for biomass projects pays approximately ¥24/kWh (~16 USD¢/kWh). *(secondary/vendor only)*

**Refined:** Japan's FIT/FIP for biomass power pays **¥24/kWh (~$160/MWh) for imported general woody biomass (wood pellets, PKS, palm trunk) in sub-10 MW projects** — rate maintained unchanged through FY2025-26 (METI, March 2025). Legacy large plants contracted 2012–2016 also hold ¥21–24/kWh contracted rates for their 20-year terms (vintage effect). New large-scale (≥10 MW) projects face competitive FIP tender with a ¥14/kWh ceiling in FY2024 (no successful bids since FY2021-22). Combined with R3 (FY2026 discontinuation for ≥10 MW), the Japan-export arbitrage advantage is structural for the legacy fleet and shrinking for the new-build pipeline.

## Refinement 5 — PP1.3-3 (Biomass moisture vs. fuel consumption)

**Original:** Every 10 percentage-point increase in biomass moisture content raises industrial boiler fuel consumption by 8–12%. *(single source: vendor blog)*

**Refined:** Every 10 percentage-point increase in biomass moisture content (wet basis) reduces the **as-received heating value by approximately 10–14%** (NREL/TP-570-33811: 10% MC wood = 7,500 BTU/lb; 30% MC = 6,000 BTU/lb; 50% MC = 4,500 BTU/lb) and reduces **boiler thermal efficiency by a further 2–5 percentage points** through increased latent-heat losses in flue gas (NREL/TP-6A20-52688 applying Babcock & Wilcox Btu Combustion Method; IEA Bioenergy Task 32 / Verenum 2006). Combined, this raises **fuel mass consumption per unit useful heat by approximately 15–25% per 10 pp moisture increase** over the typical 20–40% MC operating range. The relationship is non-linear and rises at higher moisture levels — the original 8–12% figure captures only the lower bound and the as-received heating value effect. Boiler-type dependent (stoker grate vs. fluidized bed vs. fixed grate) within this range.

**New sources:**
- (R5.1) NREL/TP-6A20-52688 (2011) Section 3.2.2 — boiler efficiency formula for moisture; https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy12osti/52688.pdf (PDF, processed via markitdown)
- (R5.2) NREL/TP-570-33811 (2004) Biomass Cofiring in Coal-Fired Boilers — heating-value-vs-MC table; https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy04osti/33811.pdf (PDF, processed via markitdown)
- (R5.3) NREL/SR-570-26946 (2000) Lessons Learned from Existing Biomass Power Plants — Kettle Falls 70% efficiency at 50% MC; https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy00osti/26946.pdf (PDF, processed via markitdown)
- (R5.4) IEA Bioenergy Task 32 / Verenum (2006) — DIN 4702 indirect method for biomass combustion efficiency; https://www.ieabioenergy.com/iea-publications/task-publications/

**Confidence after:** Medium
