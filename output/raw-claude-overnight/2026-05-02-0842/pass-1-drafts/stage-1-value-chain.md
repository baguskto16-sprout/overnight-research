## Stage 1: Fuel Suppliers

*Industrial solid-fuel package boilers — Southeast Asia*
*Research depth: WP Stage 1 / IMI value-chain reference*
*Last updated: 2026-05-02*

---

### Description

Fuel suppliers are the upstream actors who source, aggregate, process, transport, and sell the solid fuels — coal (sub-bituminous, lignite), biomass (EFB, PKS, rice husk, bagasse, sawdust, wood chips, pellets) — consumed in industrial package boilers in the textile, food and beverage, palm oil, rubber, paper and pulp, and chemicals sectors across Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand, and Vietnam.

This stage spans a highly heterogeneous set of actors: listed coal mining conglomerates (Adaro, Bayan, PT Bukit Asam) operating at multi-million-tonne scale, specialist biomass aggregators (PT Prima Khatulistiwa Sinergi, Biomassa Energi Group), vertically integrated palm mills that self-generate their own EFB/PKS, wood-pellet manufacturers (BVN Biomass, Agrideco Vietnam), and smaller B2B spot traders supplying industrial customers under short-term contracts.

---

### Activities

1. **Coal sourcing and blending:** Indonesian traders source sub-bituminous coal from Kalimantan and Sumatra mines (ICI 4–ICI 2 grades, 4,200–5,800 kcal/kg GAR), blend grades to meet customer-specified calorific value bands (e.g., target 5,000 kcal/kg GAR for textile boiler), and manage domestic market obligation (DMO) allocation with the Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources (MEMR) before sale.
2. **Biomass aggregation and drying:** EFB and PKS aggregators collect mill residues directly from palm oil processing facilities (typically at zero or near-zero feedstock cost), dry material from 60–70% raw moisture to 15–25% acceptable range, and consolidate volumes at regional stockpile points (e.g., PT Prima Khatulistiwa Sinergi's four Sumatra/Kalimantan stockpiles) for industrial-customer or export dispatch.
3. **Pelletizing and densification:** Wood-pellet manufacturers (predominantly Vietnam) procure sawdust, wood chips, and plantation forestry waste, run ring-die pellet mills to produce ISO 17225-2 compliant pellets (calorific value ~17–18 MJ/kg as-received), and dispatch finished product through dedicated port infrastructure to industrial buyers in Japan, South Korea, and to a smaller extent domestic SEA industrial users.
4. **Quality testing and certification:** Establish proximate/ultimate analysis (moisture, ash, volatile matter, fixed carbon, sulfur, chlorine) per customer or export specification; for PKS/EFB destined for Japanese FIT-regulated markets, certify Green Gold Label (GGL) or PEFC sustainability standards before shipment; for coal, reference ICI/HBA benchmark prices.
5. **Logistics coordination:** Arrange river-barge haul from inland mine/mill to coastal loading point (dominant for Indonesian coal and Kalimantan biomass), road trucking for shorter hauls (rice husk, bagasse), and sea freight to destination industrial cluster or export port; manage inventory buffers at staging stockyards to smooth seasonal feedstock availability gaps.

---

### Revenue Model

Per-tonne commodity sales on spot or short-term contract basis (30–90 days typical for industrial biomass; 3–12 months for coal under DMO-regulated agreements), with margin earned on the spread between aggregated feedstock acquisition cost and delivered price to the industrial customer; large coal miners also earn royalty-linked rents from Indonesian government HBA-priced supply.

---

### Cost Drivers

Primary costs: feedstock acquisition (zero for mill-residue EFB/PKS; market price for coal at mine gate); processing (drying, pelletizing, screening); river-barge and road logistics (dominant share of delivered cost — EFB delivered cost in Malaysia estimated at $25–45/dry tonne); inventory holding and quality-loss from moisture re-uptake in storage; sustainability certification fees (GGL for Japanese-market PKS); and Indonesian DMO compliance administration.

### WH5: Fuel Quality Variability Evidence

**EFB (Empty Fruit Bunch) — typical composition:**
- Raw moisture: 60–70% (as-received from mill); dried to 15–25% for fuel use
- Net calorific value (dry basis): ~15–17 MJ/kg; as-received (60% moisture): ~6–8 MJ/kg
- Ash content: 7–9.4% (dry basis)
- Chlorine: 0.21–0.95 wt% — elevated, causes boiler corrosion concerns (36)
- Sulfur: ~0.04 wt% — low (36)

**Rice husk — typical composition:**
- Ash content: 15–20% — high; ash >75% silica, fusion temperature ~1,500°C (37)
- Causes sticky silica deposits, grate blockage in stoker boilers (37)
- Calorific value: ~14–15 MJ/kg (dry basis)

**PKS:**
- Calorific value: ~18–20 MJ/kg (dry basis)
- Chlorine: low; more favorable combustion characteristics than EFB
- Moisture: 12–20% as-received

**Coal (sub-bituminous, Indonesian ICI 4 grade):**
- Calorific value: 3,800–4,200 kcal/kg (15.9–17.6 MJ/kg) GAR
- Variability arises from blending; customer complaints when delivered grade falls below contracted specification (38)

### Preliminary Pain Points (this stage — fuel supplier's perspective)

1. **Feedstock seasonality and weather risk:** Biomass supply is highly seasonal; monsoon-driven wet seasons (Oct–Jan in Sumatra/Kalimantan) cause moisture spikes in stockpiled rice husk, EFB, and wood chips, forcing quality claims or contract renegotiation with industrial buyers who specified a moisture maximum. (Evidenced: ASEAN bioenergy logistics review notes "seasonality and unreliability of biomass supply" as a principal commercial barrier.) (39)

2. **Export-pull diverting domestic supply (Vietnam pellets):** ~94–96% of Vietnam's 6 Mt+ annual pellet output is absorbed by Japanese and South Korean FIT-program buyers paying long-term contract premiums ($145–165/t Japan vs. domestic industrial spot ~$80–100/t estimated), leaving domestic SEA industrial boiler operators unable to secure reliable pellet supply at competitive price — price-pull structural tension documented in Vietnam industry press. (12)(14)(33)

3. **Quality variability generating customer claims:** EFB as-received moisture varies between mills (60–70%) and by season; if aggregator drying is insufficient or storage exposure occurs, delivered moisture can exceed boiler operator's specification, causing combustion instability and triggering contract disputes. Documented moisture range issue specific to EFB biomass supply chains. (36)

4. **DMO regulatory friction (coal):** Indonesian coal suppliers must comply with the 25% Domestic Market Obligation and a domestic price cap ($70/t for DMO coal, rising to $90/t for industry under MEMR regulation) — a significant discount to export spot prices during high-market periods, compressing margins and creating incentive to minimize domestic sales volumes while remaining compliant. (3)(40)

5. **Indonesia EFB aggregation logistics cost:** EFB from remote Kalimantan/Sumatra mills requires river barge haulage before sea freight, adding $15–25/t in logistics cost per aggregator LCA studies, making delivered EFB only marginally competitive against coal in some industrial applications; aggregators face pressure to either locate closer to mills or absorb margin compression. (7)

6. **PKS sustainability certification friction:** Japan's April 2024 FIT mandate for GGL-certified PKS created a two-tier market — certified (premium, stable) vs. non-certified (spot, more volatile); Indonesian and Malaysian aggregators without certification infrastructure face exclusion from the highest-price export channel and must sell into lower-margin industrial spot markets. (10)(28)

7. **Silica fouling and ash-related customer claims from rice husk:** Rice husk's 15–20% silica-rich ash causes grate fouling and deposit formation in industrial boilers; suppliers of rice husk fuel face disputes when boiler performance degrades — particularly with operators who blended rice husk into coal-fire boilers not designed for high-silica fuel. (37)

8. **Smallholder aggregation friction:** Agricultural biomass feedstocks (rice husk, bagasse) are sourced from thousands of geographically dispersed smallholder farms and small mills; aggregation logistics and quality consistency across diverse micro-lots is costly; no centralized market platform or digital aggregation infrastructure identified in open sources for this market. (41)
