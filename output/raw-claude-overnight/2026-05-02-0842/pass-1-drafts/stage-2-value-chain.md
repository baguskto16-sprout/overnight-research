## Stage 2: Boiler OEMs / Package Boiler Manufacturers

### Description

Boiler OEMs in this context are companies that design, fabricate, type-test, and sell solid-fuel industrial package boilers — factory-assembled steam-generating units (skid-mounted or shipping-container-modular, 0.5–25 t/h steam equivalent) fired by coal, biomass (PKS, EFB, rice husk, bagasse, wood chips/pellets), or agricultural residues. They sell finished pressure-vessel-plus-ancillary packages to industrial end users in the food, palm oil, textile, rubber, paper, and chemical sectors. They go to market via direct sales teams, local agents/EPC sub-contractors, and in-region service subsidiaries.

---

### Activities

1. **Process and combustion design** — select grate technology (chain grate, reciprocating grate, step grate, FBC/CFB) and size combustion chamber and furnace for the specified fuel's CV, moisture, and ash characteristics; design superheater layout if required.
2. **Pressure-part fabrication** — manufacture steam drum, water/fire tubes, headers, and associated pressure parts under ASME Section I or local code equivalent (DOSH Malaysia, DIW/DOEB Thailand, Disnaker Indonesia, MOLISA Vietnam); perform radiography, hydrostatic test, and third-party stamp approval.
3. **Sub-assembly integration** — factory-fit refractory lining, insulation, fuel-feed system (chain conveyor, screw feeder, spreader stoker), fly-ash/bottom-ash extraction, ID/FD fans, basic instrumentation, and interconnecting piping to make a true package (pre-piped, pre-wired) before shipment.
4. **Country type-approval and certification** — register designs with relevant national pressure-vessel authority (Migas/Disnaker Indonesia; DOEB/DIW Thailand; DOSH Malaysia; MOLISA Vietnam); obtain statutory certificates required before commissioning at end-user site.
5. **Logistics and site commissioning** — ship factory-assembled package (container or break-bulk), supervise installation, cold and hot commissioning, first-fire, and steam-to-process handover; provide 12–24 months warranty service and spare-parts supply.
6. **Operator training and aftermarket** — train customer engineers on startup/shutdown procedures, combustion tuning, and ash-handling maintenance; supply proprietary spares (grate bars, refractory, fan impellers) through agent network.

---

### Revenue Model

One-time capex equipment sale per unit ($200K–$2M+ per package depending on capacity and technology tier) plus low-margin recurring warranty-period parts supply and optional extended-service contracts; Indian and Chinese OEMs primarily capture the equipment sale; European OEMs increasingly bundle long-term service agreements.

---

### Cost Drivers

Boiler-grade steel for pressure parts (SA-516, SA-210 tubes — typically 35–45% of ex-works cost); labor for fabrication and welding (significant for Indian/SEA local OEMs, lower for Chinese given scale); refractory material and lining labor; fuel-feed mechanism complexity (variable-speed grates and spreader-stokers for biomass add cost vs. coal chain grate); ID/FD fans and motors; third-party inspection and stamp costs per country; warranty reserves (higher for biomass units due to fuel variability); freight and import duties.

### Preliminary Pain Points (this stage)

- **Margin compression from Chinese competition.** Chinese OEMs (ZBG, Zozen, Taishan) price chain-grate coal/biomass packages at 20–35% below Indian OEM equivalents, forcing Indian OEMs to discount or differentiate on aftersales — a difficult proposition in capex-driven SEA procurement where purchase price dominates TCO calculations. (15), (16), (17)
- **Warranty cost exposure from fuel quality mismatch.** Biomass moisture variability (EFB up to 60–70%, requiring drying to 15–25% for efficient combustion) and non-standard CV cause combustion instability, accelerated refractory wear, and tube fouling; warranty claims that arise from operator using off-spec fuel are technically the customer's fault but commercially difficult to reject without damaging the relationship. (Stage 1 context; sourced from biomass boiler technical literature reviewed above)
- **Long sales cycles and capex-deferral risk.** Industrial capex decisions in SEA palm oil, textile, and food sectors are deferred during commodity price downturns (CPO price weakness, paddy price caps); typical order-to-delivery is 10–16 months for a custom package; OEMs carry engineering and fabrication pre-investment risk on letters of intent that may be cancelled. [ASSUMED-4]: OEM average sales cycle (first contact to PO) in SEA solid-fuel market is 9–18 months — to validate: interview sales directors at Thermax and Boilermech SEA divisions.
- **Tightening emissions standards forcing combustion redesign.** Malaysia (Emissions Reg 2014 update) and Indonesia (Permen LHK No. 15/2019 on industrial boilers) require ESP or baghouse for solid-fuel boilers >capacity threshold; Thailand's DEDE incentive program pushes higher efficiency. An ESP/baghouse adds $30K–$150K to a package and requires OEM to redesign flue-gas path and fan sizing, raising engineering cost per order. (26)
- **FX exposure for Indian and Chinese OEMs.** Contracts with SEA buyers are typically in USD but domestic costs are in INR or CNY; IDR, THB, MYR, and VND can depreciate significantly during currency stress events (e.g., IDR -15% during 2022 commodity cycle), eroding margin on contracts denominated in local currency or where payment delays create FX conversion losses. [ASSUMED-5]: Indian OEMs absorb most FX risk on SEA contracts rather than transferring to buyers — to validate: ask Thermax/ISGEC treasury teams about hedging practice for SEA contracts.
- **Thin aftermarket network in SEA.** Most Indian and Chinese OEMs rely on 1–2 regional agents rather than dedicated in-country service centers; grate bars, refractory bricks, and fan impellers are not stocked locally; emergency replacement lead times of 8–16 weeks are common, causing extended downtime for end users and warranty liability exposure for OEMs. [ASSUMED-6]: Average spare-parts emergency lead time from Indian OEMs to Indonesian/Thai customers is 6–12 weeks — to validate: interview palm oil mill maintenance managers in Sumatra or Johor.
- **Variable biomass fuel-feed and ash-handling design complexity.** Biomass fuels (EFB, rice husk, wood chips) vary in bulk density, particle size, and silica content — requiring more complex fuel-feed systems (live-bottom bins, variable-speed screw feeders, spreader-stokers) and high-silica refractory for rice husk applications; OEM design libraries built for coal do not transfer directly, requiring additional engineering effort per order. (Stage 1 context; sourced from EPCB technical literature above)
- **Increasing emissions-package integration burden.** As ESP and baghouse become effectively mandatory in Malaysia and Indonesia for new installations, OEMs must integrate a third-party air pollution control system into the package design; this creates coordination risk (interface between pressure parts supplier, ESP supplier, and civil contractor), extends commissioning timelines, and increases OEM liability if the integrated system fails to meet emissions targets. (26)
