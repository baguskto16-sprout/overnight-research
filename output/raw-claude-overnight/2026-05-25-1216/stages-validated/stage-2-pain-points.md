# Stage 2 Pain Points: Indonesian Third-Party Administrators (TPAs)

*Run ID: 2026-05-25-1216 | Pass 1 draft | Researched: 2026-05-25*
*Stage actor: Indonesian health TPAs — AdMedika (Telkom Group, market leader, 5.1M+ members), Owlexa Healthcare (Lippo/Lintasarta-affiliated), Meditap (PT Teknologi Pamadya Analitika, independent), Fullerton Health Indonesia, HealthMetrics Indonesia (formerly Across Asia Assist), Global Excel Indonesia, eMHA, Globalmed. The intermediary that touches every claim in the corporate channel and either surfaces or hides the metabolic cost burden.*

## Stage 2 — Indonesian Third-Party Administrators (TPAs)

---

### Pain point 2.1

**Description:** TPA standard reporting packages deliver benefit-category aggregates — inpatient, outpatient, pharmacy, dental — to employer clients, not condition-level or member-cohort views. Diabetes, hypertension, and dyslipidemia are not extractable as cost cohorts from a standard employer utilization report. A diabetic employee's outpatient visits, pharmacy spend, and eventual inpatient complication arrive in three separate benefit-category line items with no diagnosis linkage between them. The metabolic disease burden accumulates as an invisible cross-category cost that never surfaces as a signal the employer can act on, until an inpatient complication lands as a surprise.

**Who bears it:** Corporate employer HR and finance functions (cannot quantify or respond to the metabolic cost driver); downstream — employees with unmanaged metabolic conditions who receive no TPA-directed pathway to CDM; and the employer plan itself through repricing that cannot be attributed to an addressable cause.

**Estimated cost:**
- Employer-sponsored inpatient cost per episode at private hospitals: Rp 22 million / USD 1,419 average per inpatient claim (MMB Indonesia HBS 2024, n=470+ companies). Metabolic complication episodes (acute MI, stroke, diabetic ketoacidosis, CKD requiring dialysis) are materially higher — Rp 100–400 million / USD 6,450–25,800 per episode — yet arrive without any flag that the underlying condition was the causal driver (1).
- Indonesia's total employer-sponsored health insurance claims grew 25% YoY in 2023 (Rp 20.83 trillion / USD 1.32 billion, AAJI data). Of this, cardiovascular and metabolic complications are the primary driver of the high-cost-claimant tail. MMB Asia survey: 87% of Asian insurers cite high-cost claimants as a top affordability concern; the leading conditions are circulatory system and metabolic disease — yet condition-level attribution is not a default TPA output (2).
- For a 1,000-employee corporate population at 11.7% diabetes prevalence (IDF): ~85 undiagnosed diabetics generate no condition-level flag in TPA reports until a complication hospitalization occurs. At Rp 22M average inpatient cost per episode (MMB HBS 2024), the 85-person unmanaged cohort represents a multi-hundred-million Rupiah inpatient risk that is unquantifiable by the employer because it sits distributed across outpatient/pharmacy/inpatient category lines (3).
- Fullerton Health Indonesia states its TPA platform can produce "more than 20 utilization reports," but describes these as utilization analytics (cost per hospital, cost per care type), not condition-cohort analytics. The gap between "utilization reporting" and "metabolic disease cohort reporting" is structural, not technical (4).

**Willingness to pay:**

**Frequency:**
- Indonesia TPA market: AdMedika alone serves 177 clients and 5.1 million+ members; Fullerton Health Indonesia covers 1,400+ provider facilities; HealthMetrics Indonesia (formerly Across Asia Assist) serves insurers including Allianz, AXA, and Tokio Marine as well as local corporates. These three players alone cover the majority of the formal-sector corporate population with supplemental insurance (5)(6).
- NextMSC projects the Indonesia insurance TPA market at USD 1.66 billion (2024), growing at 20.6% CAGR to USD 5.32 billion by 2030 — confirming the scale of the intermediary layer between employer and claims data (7).
- IDF Diabetes Atlas 2024: 73.2% of Indonesian diabetics undiagnosed. In the formal corporate workforce, even partial detection via MCU would produce a cohort view — but MCU data does not route back to TPA systems (see RC5 below and Pain point 1.1 from Stage 1) (8).
- No Indonesia TPA currently publicly advertises condition-level cohort reporting (ICD-10 diabetes or hypertension cohort views, longitudinal member attribution across care categories) as a standard contract deliverable. Fullerton Health's 20+ utilization reports and HealthMetrics Cloud Platform both emphasize real-time claims access and cost containment — without explicit chronic-disease cohort analytics as a default product (4)(6). **Root Causes**

- **RC1: TPA commercial contracts are scoped around claims authorization, payment routing, and benefit-category utilization review — not around population health analytics — so condition-level reporting is not a default deliverable.** The primary service that Indonesian TPAs sell is cashless claims authorization and reimbursement processing: the TPA stands between the employee, the hospital, and the insurer/employer, validates that the claim falls within the benefit design, and routes payment. This is a transactional, per-claim service. AdMedika's core product is described as "Health Claim Management and Provider Management Service." Meditap describes its company solution as administrative services and care management. Fullerton Health's standard TPA deliverable is benefit plan management, network management, claims processing, and 20+ utilization reports — all framed around cost-per-care-type, not cost-per-disease-cohort. The commercial scope has never been extended to include longitudinal member-level ICD-10 attribution across care categories because no buyer has historically specified that requirement and no TPA has built it as a standard offering. This is not a data-availability gap (ICD-10 codes are captured in hospital claims); it is a product-definition gap: the TPA's contract does not define population health analytics as a deliverable. (Sources: AdMedika about page — https://www.admedika.co.id/en/about-us; Fullerton Health TPA — https://www.fullertonhealth.co.id/services/third-party-administration/; Meditap company solutions — https://meditap.id/solutions/company)

- **RC2: Indonesian group health plans are predominantly fully-insured, meaning the insurer (not the employer) owns the diagnostic claims data and has no obligation to provide condition-level views.** Under fully-insured arrangements, the insurance company owns the claim file. TPAs under fully-insured arrangements are contracted by the insurer, not the employer. The employer receives aggregate utilization reports structured by the insurer's reporting template — which is category-level. Self-insured (self-funded) arrangements, where the employer owns the claim file and can query it directly by ICD-10 code, are common for large US employers but structurally rare in Indonesia. Khoe et al. (2024, Health Policy and Planning) found employer-sponsored health insurance coverage in Indonesia at only 36.1–38.4% of formal workers (2018–2022), with the formal employer market dominated by fully-insured group products from AXA Mandiri, Allianz Life, Manulife Indonesia, Prudential, and similar, leaving the employer on the outside of the data. The data asymmetry is therefore structurally embedded in the contract form, not just TPA design. (Source: Khoe et al. 2024 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11308606/)

- **RC3: ICD-10 diagnostic coding quality in Indonesian provider claims is variable and not verified for cohort-analytics purposes — even where TPA systems capture the code, the data is not reliable enough to build a condition-level cohort view without additional clinical review.** The Prastyo & Gani (2025) retiree insurance study, which accessed ICD-10 claim codes from a single large employer insurer, explicitly noted that "diagnosis verification was not conducted directly by the researcher — instead it was reviewed [by the employer's medical advisors]." BPJS JKN data, covering the public insurance channel, has been described by researchers as having "limited quality of diagnostic information" as a primary limitation. In the supplemental employer channel, coding quality depends on hospital billing practice and the TPA's clinical review staff. TPAs under SEOJK 7/2025 / POJK 36/2025 are now required to have qualified medical practitioners for utilization review, but this requirement was deferred from January 2026 and will be implemented under the forthcoming POJK — meaning the clinical quality assurance layer for coding was not in place for most of the history of these contracts. Without reliable condition-level coding, building a cohort view based on ICD-10 attribution is unreliable and produces false assurance. (Source: Prastyo & Gani 2025 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12425502/; Ng et al. 2019, NHI databases Indonesia — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6861401/; Nusantara Legal — SEOJK 7/2025 requirements — https://nusantaralegal.com/new-health-insurance-products-standards/)

- **RC4: The MCU data layer — where metabolic risk is first structurally detectable — is operated by a separate provider ecosystem with no standardized feed into the TPA claims environment.** Annual medical check-ups (MCU) mandated under Permenaker 5/2018 generate fasting blood glucose, cholesterol, blood pressure, and (in premium packages) HbA1c results for each employee. These results sit in the MCU provider's system (Prodia, Kimia Farma Diagnostika, hospital MCU departments). No standardized data exchange between MCU providers and employer TPAs exists. The SEOJK 7/2025 framework introduces requirements for digital data exchange between insurers, TPAs, and health facilities — but this applies prospectively to claims, not to MCU results, and was deferred until the forthcoming POJK. The gap produces a structurally incomplete view: the TPA sees none of the metabolic risk markers generated at MCU, so even an employer who buys analytics add-ons receives a view confined to claims already incurred, not to the metabolic risk profile that predicts future claims. (Source: SEOJK 7/2025 — Nusantara Legal summary — https://nusantaralegal.com/new-health-insurance-products-standards/; Permenaker 5/2018; Aon Indonesia OJK regulation insight — https://www.aon.com/apac/insights/blog/default/navigating-ojks-health-insurance-regulation)

- **RC5: No employer benchmarking survey in Indonesia currently tracks "condition-level TPA reporting" as a reported metric, so employers have no normative signal that a condition-cohort view is purchasable or that peers are using one.** MMB, WTW, and Aon Indonesia publish annual benefits benchmarking surveys covering benefit prevalence (inpatient, outpatient, dental, vision, mental health) and medical trend rates, but not the analytical capabilities of their TPA contracts. An HR director reviewing their peers' benefits practices has no external reference point showing that "X% of peer companies can query their claims data by ICD-10 chronic disease cohort." The absence of benchmarking normalizes the category-level reporting gap. To validate: request survey instruments from Marsh Indonesia, WTW Indonesia, Aon Indonesia.

### Pain point 2.2

**Description:** TPA fee structures in Indonesia are built around claims transaction volume — per-claim processing fees and PMPM administrative fees that scale with the number of claims authorized and processed. A TPA that successfully supported an employer's CDM program and reduced metabolic-disease-related claims by 15% would reduce its own fee revenue. This is not a marginal or theoretical incentive problem: it is the structural consequence of pricing a service per claim when the desired outcome is fewer claims. No commercially deployed Indonesian TPA currently operates under a shared-savings, outcomes-based, or avoided-claim fee model that would align TPA revenue with prevention success. Tests WH4.

**Who bears it:** Employer clients (cannot purchase a prevention-aligned TPA contract because the product does not exist as a standard market offering); employees (no TPA-funded CDM pathway or metabolic risk routing); and the broader employer health plan cost trajectory (claim-prevention ROI is structurally unrealizable through the current TPA channel).

**Estimated cost:**
- TPA administrative fees in Indonesia are not publicly disclosed at a per-claim level. In the US market, TPA PEPM/PMPM fees typically range from USD 15–50/member/month depending on service scope; per-claim processing fees for a high-volume book at USD 1–5/claim are industry norms (reference: Thomson Reuters health plan cost data; global TPA market benchmarks). These structural norms confirm that TPA revenue scales with claim volume, not with claim reduction. An Indonesian TPA serving a 10,000-member corporate book at even a conservative per-claim structure earns more in a high-utilization year than a low-utilization year — creating a structural revenue disincentive against recommending or delivering prevention programs. To validate: request specimen contracts from large corporate TPA clients (Astra, Bank BRI, PLN, Telkom Indonesia).
- The MMB HTS 2025 report documents that globally, only 4 in 10 insurer plans include preventive cancer screening by default; preventive metabolic screening inclusion is explicitly described as even lower. In Indonesia, the 19% medical trend (MMB 2025) and 87% of Asian insurers citing high-cost claimants as top concern are consistent with a market in which prevention investment is structurally underperforming — a pattern attributable in part to the claim-throughput revenue model (1).
- Roundstone Insurance's chronic disease management analysis (US self-funded context, 2026) states explicitly that in fully insured plans, "effective chronic disease management creates a perverse incentive: if you successfully lower claims by keeping employees healthy, the carrier keeps the savings and likely still raises your rates." In Indonesia, the same dynamic operates at the TPA level: TPA revenue scales with claims, and prevention savings accrue to the employer/insurer but not to the TPA. This is the structural basis for WH4. (2)

**Willingness to pay:**

**Frequency:**
- The Indonesia TPA market processes claims for 177+ corporate clients at AdMedika alone, operating under standard fee structures with no publicly disclosed prevention-aligned alternative (3).
- OJK's 2023–2027 insurance industry roadmap mandates POJK compliance for TPA administrative services — but the regulatory framework focuses on claims processing standards, utilization review, and Medical Advisory Board requirements, not on prevention-aligned fee models (4).
- The Aon Indonesia analysis of SEOJK 7/2025 / POJK 36/2025 reforms describes new Medical Advisory Board requirements and utilization review standards for insurers — but the TPA fee model is not addressed; the regulatory incentive for prevention investment remains absent (5).

**Root Causes**

- **RC1: The commercial definition of "TPA service" in Indonesian regulation and industry practice equates TPA services with claims transaction processing, making prevention delivery structurally outside the TPA's contracted scope.** POJK 36/2025 defines TPA services as "administrative services including claims management, Insurance Policy management and other administrative functions." SEOJK 7/2025 introduces a Medical Advisory Board (DPM) requirement for utilization review — a reactive clinical governance function for reviewing claims already submitted — not a proactive disease management function. No Indonesian regulation or industry association standard defines CDM delivery, metabolic risk routing, or pre-complication intervention as part of TPA scope. The result: a TPA that wanted to offer CDM would be stepping outside its regulatory definition and creating liability uncertainty; a TPA that restricts itself to the regulatory definition cannot offer prevention. The structural incentive for TPA fee-model innovation is absent at the regulatory level. (Sources: Mondaq SEOJK 7/2025 analysis — https://www.mondaq.com/reinsurance/1661826/legal-insight-new-health-insurance-products-standards-ojk-circular-letter-no-7seojk052025; Lockton POJK 36/2025 — https://global.lockton.com/us/en/news-insights/indonesia-to-reform-health-insurance-products-for-enhanced-risk-management)

- **RC2: Indonesian employer group health plans reprice annually, making the 3–5 year investment horizon for prevention ROI incompatible with the annual TPA contract cycle.** CDM investment in year 1 (screening, enrolment, medication adherence support) reduces complication-related claims in years 3–5. Under annual TPA contracts, neither the TPA nor the employer can capture or share prevention ROI across a multi-year period: the TPA contract may not renew, the employer's insurer may change, or the employer may restructure its plan. This is not an execution problem — it is a structural incompatibility between the CDM payback period and the contract duration. A TPA has no rational basis for investing in prevention infrastructure it cannot recover through its fee across a 12-month contract. (Source: MMB HTS 2025 — https://www.fwd.co.id/files/v3/assets/blt0c90459332411c22/bltea93d95ff78d64c5/67b8270218515989b9a6916c/Mercer_Marsh_Benefit_Health_Trend_2025_(1).pdf; Roundstone CDM analysis — https://roundstoneinsurance.com/blog/chronic-disease-management/)

- **RC3: No Indonesian insurer, regulator, or employer association has established a shared-savings or outcomes-linked payment benchmark for TPA contracts, so even a willing TPA and employer cannot reference a precedent or standard to structure an alternative fee model.** In some mature markets (US self-funded sector, parts of Singapore), value-based contracting and shared-savings structures between employers, TPAs, and vendors have been tested. No comparable precedent exists in the Indonesian market. The OJK's 2023–2027 roadmap addresses TPA regulatory compliance and insurer–TPA coordination but does not introduce incentive alignment requirements. Without a benchmark contract structure, legal precedent, or regulatory recognition, the transaction cost of negotiating a novel performance-linked fee model is prohibitive for most employer HR functions, which lack actuarial capacity to design or evaluate such arrangements. (Sources: OJK Roadmap 2023–2027 — https://ojk.go.id/en/berita-dan-kegiatan/info-terkini/Documents/Pages/Roadmap-for-the-Development-and-Strengthening-of-the-Indonesia-Insurance-Industry-2023-2027/Roadmap%20for%20the%20Development%20and%20Strengthening%20of%20the%20Indonesia%20Insurance%20Industry%202023-2027.pdf; [ASSUMED-5]: Has any Indonesian insurance regulator, employer association, or industry body proposed a prevention-linked TPA fee model standard? To validate: search AAJI, AAUI, DJSN regulatory outputs 2022–2025.)

- **RC4: The Prolanis CDM program (BPJS-funded, primary-care-anchored) operates entirely within the BPJS JKN channel, with no contractual interface to the supplemental employer TPA channel — ensuring that even the best-practice Indonesian CDM model is structurally invisible to the TPAs that serve formal-sector employer populations.** Prolanis serves BPJS-enrolled patients with diabetes and hypertension at primary care centers (Puskesmas, GP clinics). A formal-sector employee is simultaneously enrolled in BPJS (via mandatory employer contribution) and in a supplemental employer plan (via the employer's TPA). Prolanis activity for that employee — quarterly HbA1c monitoring, diabetes club attendance, medication adherence tracking — generates data in the BPJS system only. The employer's TPA sees none of it. As of 2024, the COB (Coordination of Benefits) framework between BPJS and private insurers was only officially launched in September 2024 (Ministry of Health KMK 1366/2024), and then only for cost-difference coordination, not for clinical data exchange. The structural separation between BPJS CDM data and supplemental TPA data means CDM outcomes are invisible to the TPA channel, and the TPA has no mechanism to either reinforce or build upon Prolanis activities. (Sources: Ministry of Health COB launch September 2024 — https://www.badankebijakan.kemkes.go.id/en/penataan-kebijakan-selisih-biaya-melalui-koordinasi-antar-penyelenggara-jaminan-dalam-program-jkn-resmi-diluncurkan/; Jakarta Post — COB scheme prolonged discussions 2023 — https://www.thejakartapost.com/business/2023/12/18/private-insurers-want-limits-to-scope-of-public-healthcare-coverage.html; Febriyanti et al. 2025 — Prolanis challenges scoping review — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12538241/)

### Pain point 2.3

**Description:** TPA network management in Indonesia is built around price negotiation with hospitals and clinics — securing tariff discounts and cashless payment arrangements — not around routing patients with identified metabolic conditions to appropriate CDM pathways. A TPA's provider network is a financial instrument for cost containment, not a clinical tool for directing high-risk members to preventive services. There is no standard pre-authorization flag in the Indonesian TPA system for "diabetic patient who has not had HbA1c in 12 months" or "hypertensive patient on multi-drug regimen presenting to ER." The network functions as a payment corridor, not a care management system.

**Who bears it:** Employers (network management generates no CDM routing value despite its administrative cost); employees with unmanaged metabolic conditions (no TPA-directed referral to CDM services when presenting to network providers); and ultimately the plan (undetected metabolic progression generates high-cost inpatient episodes that a CDM pathway would have deferred or prevented).

**Estimated cost:**
- Fullerton Health Indonesia advertises "cost control" and "appropriate medical check-ups for members" through analytics — but network management is described as negotiating contracted facilities and directing members to in-network care, not as routing chronic-disease patients to CDM programs (1).
- In mature markets (US, Singapore, UK), TPAs with chronic disease management programs have demonstrated ROI: Roundstone's CDM analysis finds that self-funded plans with active CDM reduce per-member claims by targeting the 20% of members who generate 80% of costs. At Indonesia's 19% medical trend (MMB 2025), an employer that could reduce the metabolic-disease portion of cost trend by 3–5 percentage points on a Rp 25.6B annual claims budget (1,000-employee reference employer from Stage 1) would save Rp 768M–1.28B / USD 49,500–82,500/year (2). These savings are structurally unavailable through the current Indonesian TPA model.
- The direct cost of unmanaged T2DM with complications in Indonesia — inpatient care is 56% of total direct medical cost (BPJS-based cost-of-illness 2016 study) — confirms that CDM pathway routing would have its largest impact on the highest-cost claims category. The network management function, if it included CDM routing, would be the natural lever to address this (3).

**Willingness to pay:**

**Frequency:**
- All major Indonesian TPAs describe their network management as focused on: provider contracting/tariff negotiation, cashless arrangement, and claims authorization at in-network facilities. None publicly describes a protocol for routing metabolic high-risk members to CDM programs within the network (1)(4)(5).
- SEOJK 7/2025 introduces managed care scheme requirements (tiered access from GP to specialist to sub-specialist), but the tiered referral is a cost-containment gatekeeping mechanism, not a disease-management routing protocol. The regulation does not define a pre-authorization protocol for metabolic risk management (6).
- Milliman Indonesia life insurance newsletter (Q4 2023): health claims increased 33% over the Q3 2022–Q3 2023 period — the fastest-growing claims segment. This is consistent with inpatient metabolic complications driving the health claims surge — a trend that CDM network routing would address but that the current network management model cannot (7).

(10) Indonesia TPA types — Meeta Consultant (2023) — TPA role defined as: claims administration, policy management, periodic employer reports ("laporan berkala"); no CDM or metabolic routing function described: https://meetaconsultant.com/berbagai-jenis-third-party-administrator-tpa-di-indonesia/

**Root Causes**

- **RC1: TPA provider contracts are built around tariff discounts and cashless payment infrastructure, not around clinical service delivery protocols — so the network has no mechanism for CDM routing even where CDM-capable providers exist in it.** A TPA's Provider Management Service negotiates hospital tariff schedules, maintains the cashless approval system, and monitors provider billing patterns for fraud/overcharge. The contract between TPA and hospital is a financial agreement: the hospital agrees to accept the TPA's authorized rates and the TPA guarantees payment. It is not a clinical services agreement specifying that the hospital's endocrinology or diabetes clinic will receive referrals based on member metabolic risk flags. To build a CDM routing function, the TPA would need to: (a) have metabolic risk data for each member (which it lacks — see Pain point 2.1), (b) have contractual agreements with CDM-capable providers to receive and prioritize metabolic referrals (which does not currently exist), and (c) have a pre-authorization workflow for CDM enrolment rather than just for inpatient hospitalization. All three prerequisites are absent. (Sources: AdMedika about page; Fullerton Health TPA; Meditap — https://meditap.id/solutions/company)

- **RC2: Indonesian TPA pre-authorization protocols are episode-based (inpatient admission, high-cost procedure), not condition-based (member has metabolic condition with lapsed monitoring) — so metabolic disease management gaps are structurally invisible to the authorization workflow.** A TPA's standard pre-authorization trigger is an event: the employee calls the TPA call center requesting a guarantee letter for a hospital admission, or a hospital submits a claim for a costly procedure. At that point, the TPA checks eligibility and benefit coverage. There is no equivalent trigger for the absence of an event: a diabetic employee who has not visited a clinic in 12 months generates no authorization request and no flag. The pre-authorization workflow is reactive and episode-triggered; a CDM routing protocol would need to be proactive and cohort-triggered. This inversion of logic requires a fundamentally different care management product, not an extension of the existing pre-authorization workflow. [ASSUMED-6]: Do any Indonesian TPAs currently operate any form of population risk stratification that generates outbound contact to high-risk members between claims events? To validate: interview Medical Advisory Board leads at AdMedika and Fullerton Health Indonesia.

- **RC3: The Indonesian provider network does not yet have sufficient CDM infrastructure outside BPJS-contracted Puskesmas to be used for a TPA-directed corporate metabolic CDM pathway.** Indonesia's specialist availability is limited: the WHO HRH profile (2024) documents significant geographic maldistribution of specialist physicians. Endocrinologists — the primary specialist for complex T2DM management — are concentrated in major hospitals in Tier-1 cities. For a corporate population TPA serving a mid-market employer with employees across Tier-1 and Tier-2 cities, constructing a CDM pathway that involves specialist referral is not feasible at scale. The clinically appropriate CDM anchor for an Indonesian corporate population is the GP-level occupational health or corporate clinic (see Stage 5), not the hospital specialist. Until GP-anchored CDM clinic models are sufficiently standardized and credentialed to be included in a TPA network as a defined CDM provider type, the network management product cannot support CDM routing. (Source: WHO Indonesia HRH Profile 2024 — https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/searo/his/hrh-profiles/indonesia_hrh_countryprofile_final_09232024.pdf; Febriyanti et al. 2025 Prolanis challenges — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12538241/)

### Pain point 2.4

**Description:** The same employee's BPJS-funded primary care encounters (including any Prolanis CDM activity), employer-GMI-funded supplemental plan claims, and MCU results sit in three structurally disconnected data environments. The Indonesian TPA serving the employer's supplemental plan sees only the supplemental claims. BPJS clinical data — including Prolanis enrollment, HbA1c monitoring, medication dispensing at Puskesmas — is not shared with the supplemental insurer or TPA. MCU results are held by the MCU provider. The TPA is therefore managing an incomplete view of the member's health status — not by negligence but because no interoperability layer exists. This is a systemic architecture failure, not a single TPA's product gap.

**Who bears it:** All actors in the chain bear a cost: the TPA cannot offer meaningful condition-level analytics or CDM routing because it lacks the data; the employer receives an artificially narrow view even if it buys analytics add-ons; employees in the Prolanis program continue unmanaged in their supplemental plan because the two systems never communicate; and BPJS CDM investment in primary care is not reinforced by the supplemental channel that could support higher-level interventions.

**Estimated cost:**
- BPJS COB (Coordination of Benefits) scheme: analysts had urged finalizing the COB framework since 2014; it was only formally implemented via KMK 1366/2024 (Ministry of Health, September 2024) — and only for cost-difference allocation, not for clinical data exchange. The 10+ year coordination failure between BPJS and supplemental insurers/TPAs means the data gap has been structurally entrenched for the entire history of the Indonesian dual-coverage system (1).
- The BPJS-K longitudinal sample data (1% of insured population, available from 2019) is available only for research purposes and is explicitly noted to lack linkage to socioeconomic data and "quality of diagnostic information." Linkage to commercial supplemental insurance data does not exist. Researchers note that "secure data linkage procedures that comply with data protection regulations could further enhance the utilisation of BPJS-K sample data" — acknowledging the absence of such procedures (2).
- Finkelstein et al. (2014, PMC4051736) estimated the NCD burden on Indonesian households at Int$4.02 billion (2010), rising to Int$5.80 billion (2020), with a substantial indirect productivity component. The structural data disconnection between BPJS and supplemental channels means this burden remains unmeasured and therefore unaddressed through either channel. To validate: BPJS Kesehatan Prolanis enrollment data by segment; cross-reference with employer insurance survey data from NLFS.

**Willingness to pay:**

**Frequency:**
- As of December 2023, 95%+ of the Indonesian population is enrolled in JKN (BPJS). Formal-sector employees at 500+ HC employers are also enrolled in employer supplemental plans via a TPA. Every one of these employees is operating across the BPJS and supplemental data silos simultaneously (3).
- BPJS Prolanis: a 2020 study (PMC6959571) found the program was operational across primary care centers nationally for T2DM and hypertension, with positive effects on metabolic control. But Prolanis data stays in BPJS systems; it does not reach the supplemental TPA channel (4).
- The OJK 2023–2027 roadmap identified "public-private cooperation" as a priority and required coordination between BPJS Kesehatan and commercial insurers by 2025 (5). POJK 36/2025 / SEOJK 7/2025 mandated coordination of benefits (COB) implementation. As of 2026, the COB scheme addresses cost coordination (who pays what) — not clinical data exchange. The data silo persists under the regulatory framework in force.

**Root Causes**

- **RC1: BPJS JKN operates a centralized single-payer database with no API or data-sharing agreement enabling commercial supplemental insurers or their TPAs to access member-level clinical encounter data.** The BPJS-K database is a government administrative system operating under health data privacy protections. Access to individual-level BPJS claims data is available only for approved research purposes, via anonymized sample extracts. There is no commercial data-sharing agreement, API, or interoperability layer that would allow a supplemental insurer or TPA to query "what BPJS-funded primary care encounters did this employee have in the last 12 months, and were they enrolled in Prolanis?" The absence is architectural and legal: no mechanism was designed or legislated for commercial access to BPJS clinical data. COB coordination (KMK 1366/2024) addresses financial claims coordination only — who pays which cost — not clinical data sharing. (Sources: BPJS longitudinal data PMC12699834; Ng et al. 2019 PMC6861401; Ministry of Health COB launch 2024)

- **RC2: MCU results are locked in provider-side medical record systems with no standardized data exchange standard for routing structured health data to employer TPAs.** MCU providers (Prodia, Kimia Farma Diagnostika, hospital MCU departments) operate proprietary EMR or laboratory information systems. The employee consents to the MCU and receives a paper or PDF report. No FHIR-based or other standardized health data exchange protocol has been mandated or implemented for routing MCU structured data (blood glucose, HbA1c, lipids, blood pressure) to employer TPA systems. SEOJK 7/2025 introduces digital data exchange requirements between insurers and health facilities, but this applies to claims data, not to MCU pre-encounter results. The regulatory gap means MCU results — the earliest point at which metabolic risk is structurally observable — remain siloed in the MCU provider system with no pathway to the TPA's risk analytics environment. (Sources: SEOJK 7/2025 — Nusantara Legal; Aon Indonesia OJK regulation)

- **RC3: The Indonesian health information exchange ecosystem lacks the foundational infrastructure (unique patient identifier, interoperable EMR standards, data governance framework) needed to link BPJS, supplemental, and MCU data at the member level.** Indonesia's EMR landscape is highly fragmented: hospitals use different systems (SIMRs, SIMRS), labs use different LIS platforms, and primary care centers use a mix of digital and paper records. No national unique health identifier (distinct from the BPJS membership number) enables reliable record linkage across these systems. The Telkom Health Information Exchange (Telkom HIE), in which AdMedika plays a role, represents an attempt to build this integration layer — but it is industry-specific and commercially structured, not a national health data infrastructure available to all TPAs and insurers. Until a regulated, interoperable national health information exchange is operational, clinical data will remain distributed across siloed systems. [ASSUMED-8]: What is the current status of the Telkom HIE and any Ministry of Health health information exchange initiatives, and do they include a pathway for supplemental TPA access to BPJS and MCU data? To validate: review Telkom Group health subsidiary disclosures; Ministry of Health data strategy documents.

### New pain points (not in original working hypotheses)

**Beyond the four candidates proposed:** Research confirmed all four candidates as genuine, structural pain points with sufficient evidence to ship. No candidate was dropped.

**One unexpected finding — partly confirmed, not proposed as standalone pain point:**

**HealthMetrics Indonesia's April 2025 launch** signals a market-entry of a more digitally sophisticated, analytics-forward TPA into the Indonesian market. HealthMetrics (now operating via its former Across Asia Assist subsidiary) explicitly positions its Cloud Platform as offering "real-time insights, track health trends, wellness strategies." This is partial evidence that at least one new market entrant is attempting to differentiate on analytics rather than purely on claims throughput. However: (a) HealthMetrics's products are described in marketing terms, without published evidence that condition-level cohort analytics or CDM routing are live deliverables in Indonesia; (b) HealthMetrics serves primarily large insurers (Allianz, AXA, Tokio Marine) and large corporates — not the mid-market employers (500–2,000 HC) that are this hypothesis's primary target; (c) the structural barriers identified in Pain points 2.1–2.4 (BPJS data silo, MCU data gap, fee model misalignment) apply equally to HealthMetrics unless they have negotiated specific data access that other TPAs have not. This finding slightly weakens the WH4 hypothesis (the incentive misalignment exists but may be beginning to crack at the high-end market) — but does not resolve it. Tag for Stage 2 deep-research: confirm whether HealthMetrics Indonesia's Cloud Platform delivers ICD-10 condition cohort views and CDM routing as live enterprise features in Indonesia, not just as aspirational marketing.
