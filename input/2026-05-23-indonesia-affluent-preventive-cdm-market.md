# Hypothesis input — Addressable Affluent Population for Preventive Care and CDM in Indonesia

## Topic

The size, growth, healthcare spending behaviour, and private insurance coverage of Indonesia's urban affluent and upper-middle population in Tier-1 and Tier-2 cities — assessed as the addressable market for a GP-anchored, asset-light managed services organisation (MSO) delivering structured preventive care (screening, vaccination) and chronic disease management (CDM) for diabetes, hypertension, and high cholesterol, funded through OOP, private insurance, or employer programmes.

## Hypothesis

The urban affluent population in Indonesia Tier-1 cities is large enough — and growing fast enough — to support a commercially viable preventive care and CDM platform funded through OOP, private insurance, or employer programmes, without relying on the mass market or public health system. Southeast Asia faces a chronic disease crisis with a system built for acute care: in Indonesia, 73% of adults with diabetes are undiagnosed (highest in the region), and existing CDM startups reach only already-diagnosed, already-engaged patients. The doctor remains the only structured touchpoint that already reaches the wider at-risk affluent population, and that population's spending capacity and insurance penetration determine whether a private-pay MSO model is commercially viable.

## Scope

In:
- Population sizing of affluent (top decile), upper-middle (2nd–3rd deciles), and aspiring middle (4th–5th deciles, as growth cohort only) in Indonesia Tier-1 and Tier-2 cities
- Healthcare spend per income segment with category breakdown: OOP consultations, insurance premiums, MCU, pharmaceuticals, preventive/wellness, CDM-related
- Income threshold at which preventive and CDM spending transitions from aspirational to discretionary behaviour, and what drives that transition (insurance coverage, health awareness, employer nudge, income headroom)
- Private health insurance penetration by income segment; employer-provided vs. individually purchased split; standard plan coverage (inpatient, outpatient, preventive, CDM, pharma)
- Top 5–6 insurers as reference (AXA Mandiri, Prudential, Manulife, Allianz, Cigna) including any structured preventive or CDM programmes they fund

Out:
- BPJS-reliant and mass-market segments
- Public health system delivery channels
- Brief B scope (employer/corporate cost burden of unmanaged CDM — covered separately on 2026-05-24)

## Geography

In: Indonesia Tier-1 and Tier-2 cities
Out: Rural Indonesia; other Southeast Asia markets

## Working hypotheses for pain points

1. **Affluent segment is large enough in absolute terms but under-served by structured preventive/CDM offerings** — private hospitals capture episodic spend but no one owns the longitudinal relationship.
2. **Preventive and CDM spending becomes discretionary at the upper-middle income threshold** — driven more by employer insurance and health awareness than pure income headroom.
3. **Private insurance is dominantly inpatient-oriented** — outpatient, preventive, and CDM coverage is thin or absent in standard plans, leaving an OOP-funded gap the MSO can address.
4. **Employer-provided insurance is the dominant access route for upper-middle** — individually purchased private insurance is concentrated in the affluent decile.
5. **Major insurers are beginning to pilot structured preventive/CDM programmes** — but execution is fragmented, creating a partnership opening for an MSO with the delivery layer.
