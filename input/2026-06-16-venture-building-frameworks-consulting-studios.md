# Hypothesis input — Corporate venture-building frameworks: consulting firms vs venture studios

## Topic

A deep, structured comparison of the venture-building frameworks and methodologies used by major consulting firms and pure-play venture studios — how each player builds new ventures with or for a corporate parent, where their approaches converge, and where and how they genuinely differ. "Venture building" is locked to **corporate new-business building**: incubating, designing, and launching new ventures as distinct entities or product lines, not internal features or efficiency projects. The output is a player-by-player deep dive, a convergence analysis, and a divergence analysis, all mapped to a fixed set of eight comparison dimensions, intended to inform strategy and be repurposed into presentation material.

## Hypothesis

The venture-building landscape splits cleanly along a **consultancy-versus-studio axis**, and that split — not the marketing language each firm uses — is the main finding. Tier-1 consultancies publish little real operational detail and frequently market "venture building" while actually delivering CVC, advisory, or branded versions of standard lean-startup / design-thinking technique; studios are more likely to take equity, supply dedicated operators, and carry real build-and-own risk. The substantive differences are driven by the underlying funding, risk, talent, and equity-ownership models, while many apparent differences are cosmetic (same model, different stage names). Naming and currency traps (BCG Digital Ventures → BCG X; McKinsey Leap) must be resolved against current state rather than older branding.

## Scope

In:
- **Players (consultancies):** McKinsey (Leap / Leap by McKinsey), BCG (BCG X, formerly BCG Digital Ventures / BCGDV), Bain (Next / Innovation & Business Building, incl. the 2023 acquisition of Rainmaking's APAC arm), EY (EY-Parthenon + any build offering), Deloitte, PwC, KPMG, Accenture (Song / Ventures where relevant to building)
- **Players (venture studios — label distinctly from consultancies):** Mach49, Rainmaking (independent vs Bain-absorbed APAC arm), FoundersLane, High Alpha, Pioneers / Pioneer-type studios, Idealab (foundational reference), plus any widely cited corporate venture studio with documented methodology
- **Eight fixed comparison dimensions** (the spine of all analysis): 1) stage coverage, 2) governance & operating model, 3) funding & risk model, 4) talent & staffing model, 5) IP & equity ownership, 6) success metrics, 7) exit & handover, 8) **methodology & activities — the core dimension, go deepest** (problem/pain-point identification, ideation, validation, market sizing, business/financial modelling, prototyping/build, go-to-market, named tools/techniques)
- **Three analytical tasks:** Task 1 framework deep dive per player (incl. the firm's own visual model with a direct source link to the diagram); Task 2 convergence (player×dimension matrix + prose, calling out the consultancy/studio clustering); Task 3 divergence (what each player most distinctively owns; substance-vs-marketing read)

Out:
- **Corporate venture capital (CVC)** — investing in external startups; in scope only as contrast or where a firm bundles it into a "build" offering (and flag the conflation as a finding)
- **Traditional innovation/advisory consulting** that produces no standalone venture — contrast only
- **Internal R&D / product development** within an existing business unit
- **Accelerators / incubators** hosting third-party startups without co-building, unless used as the firm's build model

## Geography

In: Global (these are global firms; report at firm level, noting regional arms such as Rainmaking APAC where the operating model differs)
Out: None specific

## Working hypotheses for pain points

2–5 candidate hypotheses the system should test against the literature:

1. **Consultancy-vs-studio is the primary divide** — studios converge among themselves (equity, dedicated operators, build-and-own) and consultancies among themselves (fee-based, secondee-staffed, build-and-advise), even where the two groups diverge from each other.
2. **Marketing-grade "frameworks" mask standard technique** — much Tier-1 material is a branded wrapper around design thinking / lean startup / jobs-to-be-done; where a "framework" is really marketing positioning rather than an actual operating model, that should be called out plainly, not presented as substance.
3. **Risk and equity ownership drive the substantive differences** — who funds the venture, who carries financial exposure, and who owns the resulting IP/equity explain the meaningful divergences; differences in stage names alone are cosmetic.
4. **Branding currency traps obscure the current state** — BCG Digital Ventures folded into BCG X and McKinsey's Leap may be rebranded, changed, or quietly dropped; methodologies must be verified against current state, with discontinued/rebranded offerings flagged.
5. **Evidence is thin and uneven** — Tier-1 firms publish little real detail; every major claim needs a source type (published methodology / marketing material / case-study inference / third-party or ex-employee account) and a confidence level (high/medium/low), with inference never presented as fact.
