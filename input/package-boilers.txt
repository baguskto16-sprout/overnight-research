# Test input for overnight-research skill — Solid-fuel package boilers

## Topic

Industrial use of solid-fuel package boilers (also called packaged boilers) — small to medium-scale boilers manufactured and assembled at a factory then shipped as a complete unit, fired by solid fuels (coal, biomass, wood pellets, husks, agricultural residues, lignite).

## Hypothesis

Operators of solid-fuel package boilers across Southeast Asian industrial sectors (textile, food processing, palm oil mills, rubber processing, paper & pulp, small-scale manufacturing) experience meaningful operational pain from two sources: the cost and volatility of solid fuel inputs, and the unreliability of the boilers themselves (downtime, maintenance frequency, ash handling, emissions compliance). These operators would pay for a service or product that materially reduces fuel cost or improves reliability, but no integrated solution exists in the regional market today.

## Scope

In:
- Industrial users of solid-fuel package boilers (capacity range roughly 0.5 to 25 tonnes of steam per hour, or equivalent thermal output)
- Solid fuel types: coal, biomass pellets, agricultural residues (rice husk, EFB, bagasse, sawdust), wood chips
- Sectors with high concentration of these boilers: textile, food and beverage, palm oil milling, rubber processing, paper and pulp, small-scale chemical and pharmaceutical manufacturing
- Value chain from fuel suppliers and boiler OEMs through to end users and aftermarket service providers

Out:
- Large utility-scale boilers (>50 tonnes steam per hour) — different value chain
- Gas-fired and oil-fired boilers — different fuel economics
- Domestic / residential boilers — different scale and buyer
- Power generation boilers (subset of utility-scale)

## Geography

In: Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand, Singapore (primary). Vietnam and Philippines as secondary if data is readily available.

Out: All other geographies for this test pass.

## Working hypotheses for pain points (to test against the literature)

1. **High fuel cost** — solid fuel prices have risen and become more volatile, especially for imported coal; biomass alternatives are inconsistent in supply and quality
2. **Reliability problems** — frequent downtime due to ash buildup, fuel feed system issues, tube fouling, or component wear; reactive maintenance dominates over preventive
3. **Emissions compliance burden** — rising regulatory pressure on PM, SOx, NOx in Indonesia and Thailand; many operators run aging boilers that struggle to meet new limits
4. **Skilled operator scarcity** — finding and retaining boiler operators with the right certifications and experience is increasingly hard
5. **Fuel quality variability** — biomass feedstock varies in moisture, calorific value, and contaminants, causing upstream operational issues

The skill should test which of these are evidenced in industry literature and which need field validation. New pain points surfaced during research should be flagged separately.
