# Hypothesis input — Cost of Undiagnosed and Unmanaged CDM in Indonesia Corporate Populations

## Topic

The quantifiable cost burden — claims, complications, and productivity loss — generated by undetected and unmanaged metabolic disease (diabetes, hypertension, hypercholesterolemia) in urban Indonesian corporate workforces, and the structure of the employer health benefits and MCU market that would have to absorb structured screening and CDM as a paid service.

## Hypothesis

Undetected and unmanaged metabolic disease in urban Indonesian corporate populations generates a quantifiable and avoidable cost burden — in claims, complications, and productivity — large enough to justify employer investment in structured screening and CDM, but currently invisible because employers lack the data to see it. A GP-anchored, asset-light MSO can surface and address this burden through structured preventive care and CDM bolted onto existing MCU and employer health benefit channels, targeting mid-to-large private-sector employers (500+ employees) outside the BPJS Prolanis pathway.

## Scope

In:
- Annual cost per unmanaged metabolic patient vs. managed patient: claims (hospitalisation, specialist visits, emergency care), pharmaceuticals, productivity loss (absenteeism, presenteeism)
- Prevalence and undiagnosed rates layered onto representative Tier-1 corporate workforces (500, 1,000, 5,000 employees) to produce implied annual cost burden ranges
- Indonesia corporate health benefits market sizing
- TPA landscape and key players (Meditap, Admedika, and peers)
- Standard MCU panel composition (inclusions/exclusions), current HbA1c inclusion rate, typical per-employee MCU spend
- Incremental cost of adding metabolic screening to an existing MCU vs. the cost burden it surfaces

Out:
- BPJS Prolanis and public health system delivery — not the target commercial channel
- Consumer / individual OOP segment (covered in Brief A on 2026-05-23)
- SME employers below 500 headcount

## Geography

In: Indonesia Tier-1 and Tier-2 cities
Out: Rural Indonesia; other Southeast Asia markets (note: Indonesia-specific cost-of-unmanaged-CDM data may be limited — draw from international literature and apply to Indonesian context with clearly stated assumptions)

## Working hypotheses for pain points

1. **Employers cannot see the metabolic cost burden in their claims data** — TPAs surface claim categories, not condition-level cohort views, so the avoidable cost stays invisible until a complication shows up.
2. **Standard MCU panels exclude or under-include HbA1c and lipid panels** — meaning even employers running annual MCUs miss the detection step for the conditions that drive the largest downstream claims.
3. **Productivity loss (absenteeism + presenteeism) is the largest unmeasured cost component** — exceeding direct claims cost for unmanaged metabolic disease, but never reported to HR or finance.
4. **TPA incentives are misaligned with prevention** — fee structures reward claim throughput, not avoided claims, so TPAs do not push employers toward structured screening or CDM.
5. **Incremental cost of adding metabolic screening to MCU is small relative to surfaced burden** — but procurement decisions sit with HR/admin teams that price MCU as a line item, not against avoided claims.
